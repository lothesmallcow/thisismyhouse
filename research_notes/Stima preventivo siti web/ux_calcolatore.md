# UX of an honest "estimate your website price" calculator (Italian local-business web studio)

> Method note: research was done on 2026-10-13 with web search only. Direct page fetching (WebFetch and curl) was blocked by the environment's egress proxy (DNS / CONNECT 403 for creacity.it, nebu.it, indicaweb.it, webfx.com). Every calculator description below therefore comes from search-engine summaries of those pages, not from walking through the tools. Question lists and result screens should be checked by hand before anyone quotes them as fact. Most conversion statistics in this field come from form-builder vendors and agencies. They are flagged **[vendor]**.

## 1. Number of questions/steps, progress indicators, question types, and gating the result behind contact details

### Takeaway
The more reliable evidence (Baymard, NN/g, Zuko benchmark data, Venture Harbour's self-reported case) points one way. Fewer, easier questions win. Splitting a necessary set of questions into short steps that start easy helps. Contact fields (email, phone) are among the most-abandoned fields. The specific lift percentages are mostly unverified vendor numbers. No independent study tests "email before result" against "result first" for price calculators. The honest default is to show the estimate first and ask for contact details only for the real quote. For an Italian studio this is also the cleanest GDPR position.

### Cited Findings
**Length / number of fields**
- Baymard: the average checkout in 2024 had 5.1 steps and 11.3 form fields (down from 11.8 in 2021 and 12.7 in 2019, per a secondary summary). Baymard argues most sites need far fewer — [Baymard, Checkout Optimization: Minimize Form Fields](https://baymard.com/blog/checkout-flow-average-form-fields); trend figures from [crodiary summary](https://crodiary.com/benchmarks/checkout-form-fields-what-baymard-s-numbers-say-to-cut/). Secondary sources disagree on the share of users who abandon because of checkout complexity: 17% in one, 22% in another. Treat that number as unverified.
- Zuko (form-analytics vendor with large aggregate data) **[vendor, but measured data]**: contact and enquiry forms have the *lowest* starter-to-completion rate of all form types. Registration forms have the highest at 60.7%. Average labelled fields: Configuration forms 21.2, Contact 19.2, Enquiry 17.4 — [Zuko form-type benchmarking](https://www.zuko.io/benchmarking/form-type-benchmarking).
- Zuko: email (6.4%) and phone (6.3%) fields have some of the highest mean field-abandonment rates, behind only password (10.5%) — [Zuko, Which form fields cause the biggest UX problems?](https://www.zuko.io/blog/which-form-fields-cause-the-biggest-ux-problems). A Reform write-up swaps the email and phone figures ([Reform](https://www.reform.app/blog/how-to-reduce-form-abandonment-insights-from-zuko-analytics)). The point holds either way: contact fields are where people leave.
- Overall completion averages depend on the definition used. One summary gives 45% view-to-completion and 66% starter-to-completion. Another cites about 51.7% across 93M sessions, attributed to Zuko — [CueFully, checked-at-source stats](https://cuefully.com/blog/form-abandonment-statistics); [Zuko 25 stats](https://www.zuko.io/blog/25-conversion-rate-statistics-you-need).
- **[vendor, unverified]** One benchmark compilation gives a median conversion of about 23% for 3-field forms and about 7% for 10-field single-step forms, or roughly −4.5 points per added field — [Digital Applied benchmarks 2026](https://www.digitalapplied.com/blog/form-conversion-rate-benchmarks-2026-data-points). Methods are not disclosed.

**Multi-step vs single-page**
- Venture Harbour (self-reported): its enquiry form went from 0.96% to 8.1% conversion (+743%) after moving to a multi-step form. Its consulting enquiry form went from 1.3% to 8.2%. Their Leadformly tool started the progress bar partly filled. Leadformly has since been discontinued — [Venture Harbour CRO case studies](https://ventureharbour.com/cro-case-studies/); [Venture Harbour / Leadformly](https://ventureharbour.com/?p=5596). This is a single company's before/after, not a controlled study.
- **[vendor, unverified]** Claims such as "multi-step 13.85% vs single-step 4.53%" and "+86% for multi-step" circulate without disclosed methods. Another vendor says that below about 8 fields a single page beats multi-step, and that gains plateau after about 3 steps — [Reform, layout & completion](https://www.reform.app/blog/research-how-layout-affects-form-completion-rates); [leadgen-economy](https://www.leadgen-economy.com/blog/multi-step-forms-conversion-optimization/); [mantasauk B2B multi-step](https://mantasauk.com/articles/multi-step-form-b2b-leads/).
- **[secondary, unverified]** A secondary source attributes to Zuko the finding that single-page forms with 16+ fields complete at about 8%, and that splitting them into 3–4 steps raises completion to 34–71%. This could not be confirmed on Zuko's own pages — [CueFully](https://cuefully.com/blog/form-abandonment-statistics).

**Progress indicators / wizard design (NN/g)**
- NN/g defines a wizard as a step-by-step flow where later steps can depend on earlier answers. Wizards suit tasks people perform rarely, which fits a once-in-years website purchase — [NN/g, Wizards](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/wizards/).
- NN/g on cognitive load: show only what is relevant to the current task. Reveal further fields progressively, through pages or conditional logic. Use progress indicators so people can judge how much effort remains. For longer forms, say the estimated completion time up front — [NN/g, 4 Principles to Reduce Cognitive Load in Forms](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/4-principles-reduce-cognitive-load/).
- NN/g (wait-time context): showing the *number of steps* can help users estimate duration better than a percentage — [NN/g, Progress Indicators](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/progress-indicators/).
- **[vendor]** "Progress bars convert 28% better" and "start the bar at 15–25%" are practitioner claims with no study behind them — [mantasauk](https://mantasauk.com/articles/multi-step-form-b2b-leads/); [Reform drop-off](https://www.reform.app/blog/multi-step-form-drop-off-rates-how-to-reduce-them).
- Underlying psychology with academic roots: the goal-gradient effect (people speed up near the finish) and foot-in-the-door (Freedman & Fraser 1966: agreeing to a small request raises compliance with a larger one). Vendors use these to argue for an easy, low-stakes first question. Free text and contact details should never come first — [heyflow micro-commitments](https://heyflow.com/blog/the-psychology-of-micro-commitments/); [Atticus Li, progressive commitment](https://www.atticusli.com/blog/posts/multi-step-forms-vs-single-page-behavioral-science-progressive-commitment/); [involve.me quiz funnel](https://www.involve.me/blog/how-to-create-a-quiz-funnel). Quiz-length advice is 5–10 questions **[vendor]**. No independent controlled tests of quiz funnels were found.

**Question types (cards, sliders, steppers)**
- The NN/g-derived guidance below is quoted in design-system docs, not NN/g directly. Sliders suit choosing an approximate range where the exact value does not matter. Use a stepper for small discrete adjustments (≤10 increments) and a number input for precise values. On mobile, many users knock the slider thumb off their value when lifting their finger, and a finger hides labels placed under the slider — [Guidewire Jutro slider docs](https://docs.guidewire.com/jutro/documentation/10.13/slider); [Guidewire stepper docs](https://docs.guidewire.com/jutro/documentation/10.10/stepper); [Semrush Intergalactic slider](https://developer.semrush.com/intergalactic/components/slider/slider). Steppers should default to the most common value and should never be blank — [Guidewire stepper](https://docs.guidewire.com/jutro/documentation/10.10/stepper); [Balsamiq steppers](https://balsamiq.com/learn/steppers/).
- **[secondary]** Forms that lean heavily on dropdowns are reported to have the highest abandonment of any field type. Zuko's underlying data was not found — [CueFully / antforms summaries](https://antforms.com/blog/reduce-form-abandonment-rate-strategies/).
- Tappable multiple-choice answers are recommended over free text for quiz-style flows **[vendor]** — [involve.me](https://www.involve.me/blog/how-to-create-a-quiz-funnel).

**Gating the result behind contact details**
- NN/g: B2B buyers see companies that hide prices as evasive and untrustworthy. For complex pricing, NN/g suggests that costs for a few *representative scenarios* often work better than a complex calculator. The article is about 8 years old, so it predates the 2019 cutoff, but it is the only research-firm source found — [NN/g, Pricing information gives B2B sites a competitive advantage](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/show-price/).
- G2: only 4% of G2 product profiles list prices explicitly. G2 argues that price acts as a gatekeeper for shortlisting and as a trust signal — [G2 Learn](https://learn.g2.com/software-pricing-transparency).
- **[vendor]** Pathmonk discusses gated vs visible pricing. Other vendor pieces suggest that if you gate an exact number, you should still publish a starting-price anchor — [Pathmonk](https://pathmonk.com/gated-pricing-demo-conversion-rate/).
- Real calculators split on this. Signalfire *emails* you the ballpark, so it is gated — [Signalfire](https://signalfire.us/how-much-does-a-website-cost/). Creacity (IT) advertises "about 1 minute, 6 questions, no email required" — [Creacity](https://creacity.it/tool/preventivo-sito-web/). Nebu (IT) runs the calculation in the browser and says no data is sent or saved — [Nebu](https://www.nebu.it/strumenti/calcolatore-costo-sito-web). CommunicAnimation sends the quote by email — [CommunicAnimation](https://www.communicanimation.com/magazine/preventivo-realizzazione-sito-web/).
- No A/B test or independent study comparing email-gated and ungated calculator results was found.

**GDPR / Italian norms for the hand-off form**
- Italian guidance: marketing consent must be a separate, optional, *unticked* checkbox, with a separate checkbox per purpose. Nothing may be pre-ticked — [My Agile Privacy, moduli di contatto](https://www.myagileprivacy.com/helpdesk/come-mettere-a-norma-gdpr-i-moduli-di-contatto/); [emailmarketingblog.it](https://emailmarketingblog.it/blog/2018/05/02/raccogliere-consensi-allepoca-del-gdpr).
- The Garante has objected to forms where consent to profiling or third-party marketing was *required* to complete registration. It also objected to a single pre-ticked box that could be unticked. That ruling dates from 2015, under the old Codice privacy art. 23, but the principle carries into GDPR — [Cristina Vicarelli, flag pre-impostati](https://www.cristina-vicarelli.it/blog/privacy-protezioni-dati-personali/web-marketing-flag-pre-impostati).
- Garante direct-marketing guidelines: closely related marketing activities (newsletters, direct sales, market research) may share one consent — [Digital4 summary of Garante guidelines](https://www.digital4.biz/executive/privacy-e-marketing-le-linee-guida-del-garante/).
- GDPR art. 7(4) says that whether consent is "freely given" depends on whether a service is made conditional on consent to processing that is not necessary for it. Recital 43 covers the same ground — [GDPR text, EUR-Lex](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2016/679/oj). This text was not fetched in this session. It is the standard reference and should be checked against the source.
- EDPB Guidelines 03/2022 on deceptive design patterns (v2.0 adopted 14 Feb 2023) are written for social media. Commentators say the dos and don'ts apply to other businesses too — [EDPB](https://www.edpb.europa.eu/our-work-tools/our-documents/guidelines/guidelines-032022-deceptive-design-patterns-social-media_en); [Deceptive Patterns summary](https://deceptive.design/articles/guidelines-03-2022-on-deceptive-design-patterns-in-social-media-platform-interfaces-how-to-recognise-and-avoid-them).

### Inferences
- **Recommended length: 5–8 questions, one decision per screen, total time under about 90 seconds.** Configuration-type forms average about 21 fields (Zuko), and calculators in the wild advertise "6 questions / 1 minute" (Creacity). Going much past 8 adds friction without making a ballpark estimate meaningfully more accurate.
- **Order:** (1) business type, an easy tap that also personalises later questions; (2) goal of the site; (3) scope or size; (4) features specific to the business model; (5) content readiness (texts and photos); (6) languages; (7) timing or urgency. The result comes next. Contact details are optional and come *after* the result, framed as "receive a firm quote / book a 20-minute call".
- Use a labelled step count ("Domanda 3 di 6") rather than a percentage bar, following NN/g's note on step counts. Avoid a falsely pre-filled bar: it would sit badly with the "honest" positioning.
- Use cards with icons and labels (native radio inputs styled as cards) for categorical choices. Use steppers for small counts (pages 1–10, rooms 1–10, languages 1–4). Avoid sliders for anything the user knows exactly, such as number of rooms. Avoid dropdowns.
- **Gating:** show the range ungated. A gated number reads as bait, and NN/g's "evasive" finding plus G2's trust argument point against it. The contact step converts better when it offers something extra: a PDF with the breakdown, a fixed-price quote within 24h, or a call. Leads arriving this way are pre-qualified on budget, which is the business value.
- GDPR-safe hand-off: the privacy notice is linked, not a mandatory "I accept" box for marketing. Use one optional, unticked marketing checkbox. Never make the quote conditional on marketing consent. If the calculator runs fully client-side, say so ("nessun dato viene inviato finché non ci scrivi"), as Nebu does. That is a trust signal in itself.

### Gaps
- No independent (non-vendor) controlled study on email-gated vs ungated calculator results was found.
- NN/g's dedicated multi-step-form article and its slider/stepper articles were not retrieved directly. The slider/stepper guidance comes via design-system docs that attribute it to NN/g.
- Baymard's abandonment-due-to-complexity figure (17% vs 22%) could not be reconciled.
- No Italy-specific data was found on form completion for SMB buyers.

## 2. Range vs single number: anchoring, precision, trust; "what's included" and "what changes the price"

### Takeaway
Pricing psychology shows that precise numbers anchor more strongly than round ones (Janiszewski & Uy 2008, which predates the cutoff but remains the foundational study). Over-precision, however, can read as incompetence or manipulation unless the number comes with a rationale. Negotiation research finds that range offers are perceived at least as well as point offers. For an indicative estimate built from coarse inputs, an honest range with a visible breakdown and explicit price drivers fits best. A falsely precise single figure would overstate what six tap-answers can know.

### Cited Findings
- Janiszewski & Uy (2008): people adjust less away from a precise anchor ($4,988) than a round one ($5,000). Field evidence: in 12,581 Florida home sales, houses listed at precise prices sold closer to list price — [Wikipedia, Precision bias](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Precision_bias) (aggregator; the original is Psychological Science 2008). APS press release on the same work: [APS, Pricing and the brain](https://www.psychologicalscience.org/news/releases/pricing-and-the-brain-why-things-cost-19-95.html).
- Mechanism debate: "scale granularity" (precise numbers imply a finer mental scale) — [Management Science 2019/2020 paper, DOI 10.1287/mnsc.2019.3570](https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/pdf/10.1287/mnsc.2019.3570). Zhang & Schwarz argue the precision advantage holds only when the speaker is presumed cooperative, i.e. when precision is read as signalling real knowledge — [Zhang & Schwarz, JESP](https://dornsife.usc.edu/norbert-schwarz/wp-content/uploads/sites/231/2023/11/13_jesp_zhang___schwarz_precise_numbers.pdf).
- Loschelder et al. (2016), 5 experiments with 1,320 participants: the precision–anchoring relationship is an inverted U for *experts*. Too much precision reads as a lack of competence, unless the first mover gives a rationale that boosts perceived competence — [Wikipedia, Precision bias](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Precision_bias).
- A later study: precise offers still reduced counteroffers even when the other party suspected a tactic, with perceived fairness mediating the effect — [Journal of Trial & Error, jts5.86](https://onlinelibrary-wiley-com-443.webvpn.zafu.edu.cn/doi/10.1002/jts5.86) (proxy mirror URL; the original is the Wiley JTE article).
- Columbia Business School (Ames & Mason, five studies): negotiators who opened with a *range* were viewed as positively as point-offer openers, or more so. "Bolstering ranges" (low end = own target) achieved higher settlements, and aggressive point offers hit impasse almost twice as often — [Columbia Business School, To Anchor a Negotiation, Two Points May Be Better Than One](https://business.columbia.edu/insights/chazen-global-insights/anchor-negotiation-two-points-may-be-better-one).
- Showing consumers a price *distribution* corrected their underestimation of price dispersion by 68% (consumer credit context) — [Higgins et al., Price comparison tools](https://seankhiggins.com/assets/pdf/PriceComparisonTools.pdf).
- NN/g recommends representative scenarios ("a typical 5-page site costs X") as a trust-building alternative to a complex calculator — [NN/g, show price](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/show-price/).
- Real-world result formats:
  - Nebu (IT) shows a line-by-line breakdown from a public price list plus a maintenance fee. Items without a standard price (e.g. more than 10 pages) are marked "su preventivo". The total is "a partire da", excluding VAT. A professional site starts from €4,000 + IVA — [Nebu](https://www.nebu.it/strumenti/calcolatore-costo-sito-web).
  - FAComunica (IT) uses base types (vetrina from €1,000, dinamico+blog from €2,500, enterprise from €5,000), €100 per page, fixed add-ons (advanced responsive +€300, basic SEO +€500, analytics +€300) and a complexity multiplier of ×0.5–×1.5. Its worked example produces a single €1,400 figure labelled indicative. The formal quote arrives by email — [FAComunica](https://facomunica.it/modulo-richiesta-di-preventivo-per-sito-web/).
  - Creacity (IT): price is driven mainly by site type, level of customisation, number of languages and features, *not* raw page count. The figure is indicative and based on the company's average prices. A final price needs a custom quote — [Creacity](https://creacity.it/tool/preventivo-sito-web/).
  - Pronto Marketing (US) uses range tiers: brochure site (1–6 pages) $2k–10k, small business (10–50 pages) $5k–20k, corporate (50–100 pages) $20k–50k, ecommerce up to $100k — [Pronto](https://www.prontomarketing.com/website-cost-calculator/).
  - Signalfire (US) bases its estimate on design complexity, page count and whether texts, photos and video are ready. It emails a "ballpark budget" — [Signalfire](https://signalfire.us/how-much-does-a-website-cost/).
  - Sayenko (US) claims the tool is built "on real pricing data from hundreds of projects" **[vendor claim]**. It directs users to a detailed quote — [Sayenko](https://www.sayenkodesign.com/how-much-does-a-website-cost/).
- Recurring costs (domain, hosting, maintenance) are often missing from headline figures. Comparison guides flag $500–5,000/yr maintenance and $15–150/month hosting/apps as commonly underestimated — [webcostestimator](https://webcostestimator.com/); [Forbes Advisor](https://www.forbes.com/advisor/business/software/how-much-does-a-website-cost/).

### Inferences
- **Show a range** (e.g. "€2.400 – €3.100 + IVA"), not one exact figure. Width should reflect actual uncertainty: roughly ±15–20% for simple sites, wider when there are unknowns such as content not ready or third-party integrations. Make the range ends slightly non-round (e.g. €2.350–€2.950). Under the precision research this anchors more credibly than "€2.000–€3.000", but the breakdown must carry the rationale so that, per Loschelder, it does not read as a gimmick.
- Pair the range with: (a) a **line-item breakdown** that sums to the midpoint or the ends; (b) **"Cosa è incluso"** (design, n pages, mobile, basic SEO, GDPR/cookie banner, training, launch); (c) **"Cosa NON è incluso / costi ricorrenti"** (domain/hosting €/year, maintenance €/month, booking-engine fee, photography, copywriting); (d) **"Cosa fa salire o scendere il prezzo"**, listing the top 3 drivers from the user's own answers ("hai scelto 3 lingue: +€X"); (e) **delivery time as a range in weeks** ("3–5 settimane dalla consegna dei contenuti"), tied explicitly to content readiness.
- State "IVA esclusa" explicitly (B2B convention in Italian calculators, e.g. Nebu, FAComunica), and the validity of prices (e.g. "listino 2026").
- Add a short "esempi tipici" block (NN/g's representative-scenario advice), e.g. "Sito per serramentista, 5 pagine, 1 lingua: €X–Y". It works as a sanity anchor even for people who skip the calculator.
- Avoid FAComunica's opaque ×0.5–×1.5 "complexity multiplier". Users cannot see what moves it, so the output looks arbitrary.

### Gaps
- No study was found that measures *trust* in range vs point estimates for service quotes (contractors, agencies). The evidence is indirect: negotiation and anchoring studies.
- Janiszewski & Uy (2008) and Loschelder et al. (2016) predate 2019. They are cited as the foundational studies because no newer replications aimed at service estimates were found.
- No research was found on the optimal *width* of a price range.

## 3. Real examples of website-cost calculators (international and Italian): questions, result screens, strengths and weaknesses

### Takeaway
Calculators fall into two families. **Transparent, ungated price-list calculators** (Nebu, Creacity, Indicaweb, FAComunica) show the number on screen. **Gated "ballpark by email" lead magnets** (Signalfire, CommunicAnimation) do not. Italian tools mostly ask about site type, pages, features, languages and customisation. None found asked business-model questions (rooms, bookings, events), which is the gap a studio serving trades, hospitality and venues can fill. (Caveat: the page-level details below come from search summaries; the pages could not be opened.)

### Cited Findings
- **Creacity (IT)**: about 1 minute, 6 questions, no email. The estimate is indicative, based on Creacity's average prices, and is followed by a custom quote. Price drivers: type, customisation level, languages, features. Creacity also offers a separate e-commerce calculator — [Creacity sito web](https://creacity.it/tool/preventivo-sito-web/); [Creacity e-commerce](https://creacity.it/tool/preventivo-ecommerce/).
- **Nebu (IT)**: built from a public price list. Professional site from €4,000 + IVA. Itemised breakdown plus maintenance fee. Out-of-standard items read "su preventivo". Result is "a partire da", excluding VAT. Runs in the browser and says no data is sent or saved — [Nebu](https://www.nebu.it/strumenti/calcolatore-costo-sito-web).
- **Indicaweb (IT)**: instant estimate from site type, pages and features, then a detailed proposal within 24h — [Indicaweb](https://www.indicaweb.it/preventivo-online/).
- **FAComunica (IT)**: base type + €/page + fixed add-ons + complexity multiplier. A single-number result, then the formal quote by email — [FAComunica](https://facomunica.it/modulo-richiesta-di-preventivo-per-sito-web/).
- **eMedia Parma (IT)**: "configuratore preventivo web e WordPress", which calculates the cost in real time. Details not retrieved — [eMedia Parma](https://go.emediaparma.it/configuratore-preventivo-web-e-wordpress-calcola-il-costo-in-tempo-reale/).
- **Webdomus (IT)**: price bands by type, e.g. a showcase site up to 5 pages from €1,500. Has a separate article on sites with an integrated booking engine — [Webdomus calcolatore](https://www.webdomus.net/calcolatore-costi-sito-web/); [Webdomus booking engine](https://www.webdomus.net/sito-web-con-booking-engine-integrato/).
- **CommunicAnimation (IT)**: quote sent by email. One-page site from €750 + IVA — [CommunicAnimation](https://www.communicanimation.com/magazine/preventivo-realizzazione-sito-web/).
- **Web2Monza (IT)**: fixed published rates, but apparently last updated in 2019. A stale price list is a trust risk — [Web2Monza](https://www.web2monza.it/costo-sito-web.html).
- Others found but not detailed: [dinamikaMente preventivo immediato](https://www.dinamikamente.net/preventivo-immediato-realizzazione-sito-web/), [SN Web Solution](https://snwebsolution.com/preventivo-sito-web.html), [Eccolo Marketing guide](https://www.eccolomarketing.it/preventivo-sito-web/).
- **Signalfire (US)**: inputs are design complexity, number of pages, and readiness of text, photos and video. The ballpark is *emailed* (gated). Typical range $6k–30k; small-business FAQ $6k–15k — [Signalfire](https://signalfire.us/how-much-does-a-website-cost/).
- **Pronto Marketing (US)**: site type and page-count tiers with ranges, plus SEO and ecommerce questions — [Pronto](https://www.prontomarketing.com/website-cost-calculator/).
- **Sayenko Design (US)**: calculator plus a long guide ($3k–50k+ for small business). Claims real data from hundreds of projects **[vendor]** — [Sayenko](https://www.sayenkodesign.com/how-much-does-a-website-cost/).
- **WebFX (US)**: calculator embedded in a long pricing guide giving a $1,000–$145,000 range — [WebFX](https://www.webfx.com/web-design/pricing/website-costs/).
- Hospitality context (IT): a booking engine is often a channel-manager module at about €30–80/month. OTA commissions average 15–30% per booking **[vendor]** — [Webdomus](https://www.webdomus.net/sito-web-con-booking-engine-integrato/). Beds24 or Little Hotelier integrate with WordPress for under €50/month. A worked example: a 5-room B&B at €80/night and 70% occupancy pays €6–10k/year to one OTA **[vendor]** — [Ikwaba](https://www.ikwaba.com/bb-asolo-prenotazioni-dirette/). Other booking-engine vendors: [Iperbooking](https://www.iperbooking.com/booking-engine.cfm), [Hotel.bb](https://www.hotel.bb/booking-engine.php), [RoomRaccoon guide](https://roomraccoon.it/risorse/booking-engine-per-hotel/), [Firenze Web Division](https://www.firenzewebdivision.it/servizi/web/realizzazione-booking-engine.html), [Gethigher](https://gethigher.it/realizzazione-siti-web-hotel-booking-engine), [Contindigital B&B guide](https://www.contindigital.it/blog/guida-sito-web-bb-agriturismi).

**What they do well and badly (synthesis of the above)**
- Done well: Creacity's "6 questions, 1 minute, no email" promise set up front. Nebu's itemised breakdown, explicit "su preventivo" for out-of-standard items, IVA-excluded label, maintenance fee shown, and client-side privacy statement. Indicaweb's concrete follow-up promise ("entro 24 ore"). Signalfire's content-readiness question, which is a real cost and timeline driver.
- Done badly: FAComunica's unexplained complexity multiplier and single-number output. Email-gated results (Signalfire, CommunicAnimation). Stale price lists (Web2Monza, 2019). Huge ranges that tell the user nothing (WebFX's $1k–$145k). Page count used as the main driver, which Creacity itself says is not what drives price.

### Inferences
**Business-model-specific question sets** (the differentiator; derived from the price drivers above plus the hospitality cost structure):
- *Common core (all):* business type (card) → main goal (more phone/WhatsApp requests / direct bookings / event enquiries / showcase) → do you have a site today? (new / redo / restyle) → texts and photos ready? (yes / partly / no: we write and photograph) → languages (stepper 1–4; IT+EN typical for tourism) → when do you need it (no hurry / within 2 months / urgent).
- *Lead-generation trades (ristrutturazioni, serramentisti):* number of services or categories (stepper), portfolio / before-after gallery (yes/no), service-area pages for towns served (stepper or "1 / 2–5 / 6+"), quote-request form with photo upload (yes/no), reviews integration (Google), incentives/bonus info pages (optional). Skip anything about bookings.
- *Hospitality with rooms (B&B, agriturismo):* number of rooms or units (stepper 1–20), direct booking: "no, only contact form / link to my existing booking engine / integrate a booking engine (recurring fee extra)", seasonal price lists or offers pages, experiences/restaurant/farm-shop section, multilingual (stronger default), map/area guide. In the result, show booking-engine monthly fees as a *recurring* line, not hidden inside the one-off price. Optionally show the OTA-commission saving as context only, clearly labelled as an estimate.
- *Event venues (wedding venues):* event types (weddings / corporate / private parties: multi-select cards), spaces/halls to present (stepper), gallery/video depth (standard / large / with video), downloadable menu or brochure, availability-check or visit-booking form, supplier partners page, multilingual for foreign couples.
- Each branch stays within about 6–8 questions in total. Branching is the only way to ask relevant questions without lengthening the flow (NN/g: later steps depend on earlier answers).

## 4. Accessibility patterns for calculators and mobile UX

### Takeaway
Build every choice as native HTML (`<fieldset>`/`<legend>` + `<input type="radio">` styled as cards, `<input type="number">` with +/− buttons, or the APG spinbutton pattern) and announce the updating estimate through a single polite live region. That covers WCAG 4.1.3 Status Messages and standard keyboard behaviour. On mobile, prefer big tap cards and steppers to sliders.

### Cited Findings
- WCAG 2.2 SC 4.1.3 Status Messages: status updates that do not receive focus (results, errors, loading) must be programmatically determinable so assistive technology can announce them without a focus change — [W3C Understanding 4.1.3](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/status-messages.html); failure technique [F103](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Techniques/failures/F103).
- Live regions are the main technique. `role="status"` equals `aria-live="polite"` and is right for a recalculating price. Reserve `role="alert"`/`assertive` for urgent errors. Use `aria-atomic="true"` so the whole result is read, and do not announce empty or redundant states — [AAArdvark 4.1.3](https://aaardvarkaccessibility.com/wcag-plain-english/4-1-3-status-messages/); [BOIA](https://www.boia.org/blog/are-your-websites-status-messages-accessible); [dock.codes 4.1.3](https://wcag.dock.codes/documentation/wcag413/).
- APG radio group: Tab moves to the checked radio (or the first one); arrow keys move focus to the next or previous radio and select it, wrapping around — [W3C APG radio example](https://raw.githack.com/w3c/aria-practices/main/content/patterns/radio/examples/radio.html); [APG radio-rating](https://w3c.github.io/wai-website/ARIA/apg/patterns/radio/examples/radio-rating/).
- APG spinbutton: the text field is usually the only focusable part. Up/Down change by a small step and Page Up/Page Down by a large step. The deprecated date-picker example is replaced by the Quantity Spin Button example — [W3C APG spinbutton pattern](https://www.w3.org/WAI/ARIA/apg/patterns/spinbutton).
- Stepper value conveyed via `aria-live="polite"` and labelled with `aria-labelledby` (design-system practice) — [Guidewire stepper](https://docs.guidewire.com/jutro/documentation/10.10/stepper).
- Mobile slider pitfalls: the thumb moves when the finger lifts, and labels sit under the finger — [Semrush Intergalactic slider](https://developer.semrush.com/intergalactic/components/slider/slider).

### Inferences
- Card choices: native radios or checkboxes inside `<fieldset><legend>Domanda 2 di 6: Che tipo di attività hai?</legend>`. The whole card is the `<label>`. Icons are `aria-hidden` with visible text. Show a visible focus ring and a checked state that does not rely on colour alone (check icon + border). Tap targets at least 44×44 px (WCAG 2.2 2.5.8 sets a 24 px minimum; 44 is the common platform guideline).
- Steps: on "Avanti", move focus to the new step's heading or legend so screen readers hear the question. Announce "Domanda 3 di 6" in that heading rather than with a decorative progress bar. Keep a working "Indietro" that preserves answers.
- Live price: one `<p role="status" aria-atomic="true">` updated with debouncing ("Stima aggiornata: da 2.350 a 2.950 euro più IVA"). Do not live-announce on every stepper click if that floods the user; announce on settle or at step change.
- The result is a real heading plus a semantic list or table for the breakdown. Any chart needs a text equivalent. Format currency for it-IT ("2.350 €" or "€ 2.350") and say "IVA esclusa" in text.
- Mobile: one question per screen, a sticky "Avanti" button, no horizontal scroll, steppers with large +/− buttons instead of sliders, `inputmode="numeric"` for typed counts, and no dropdowns for 2–6 options.
- No dark patterns at the hand-off: no fake countdowns or "only 2 slots left", no pre-ticked consent, no forced phone number (make it optional; the Zuko data shows phone is a top abandonment field), and an easy "Ricomincia" and "Scarica stima PDF" without email.

### Gaps
- No usability research was found on screen-reader experience with live-updating price calculators specifically. The guidance is extrapolated from WCAG 4.1.3 and the APG patterns.
- The NN/g slider article was not retrieved directly.
- Italian accessibility law (Legge Stanca, extended to some private companies by Law 2020/2021 and the European Accessibility Act from 28 June 2025) was not researched in this session. Whether small studios' clients fall in scope (micro-enterprise exemptions) needs checking separately.
