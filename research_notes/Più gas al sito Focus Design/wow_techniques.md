# Wow techniques: how to give the Focus Design site more "gas" without losing clarity, speed or conversions

Research note, 22/11/2026. Method: web search only. Every WebFetch call failed because the session's network rules block nngroup.com, web.dev and ddingpy.github.io. That means the findings below come from search-engine summaries of the pages, not from full-text reads. Treat exact wording and numbers as needing a re-check before anything is quoted to a client. "EVIDENCE" marks measured or tested findings. "OPINION" marks practitioner or juror views.

## 1. What award-recognised studio and portfolio sites (Awwwards, FWA, CSSDA, Godly, Siteinspire, 2023-2026) do to create excitement

### Takeaway
The recognised studio sites I could verify win on one or two strong ideas carried out well, not on many small effects. Examples are new native CSS motion (view transitions, scroll-driven animation), horizontal scroll with a minimap, or oversized type with thin weights. Jurors reportedly mark down scattered effects, weak mobile layouts, missing reduced-motion paths and custom cursors that hide the real one. This matches the owner's complaint that the site is "too heavy on small details".

### Cited Findings

Awwwards describes the site as a showcase of her work "built with the newest CSS techniques: View Transitions and Scroll Driven Animations". This is the closest named precedent for a no-framework, CSS-first build like Focus Design's Astro stack. — [Awwwards: Cyd Stumpel](https://www.awwwards.com/sites/cyd-stumpel-portfolio-2025)

### Inferences
- The pattern across the verified examples is one signature move per site, done fully. Cyd Stumpel's is native CSS view transitions plus scroll-driven animation, Rauno's is horizontal scroll plus a minimap, and the Designmodo examples rely on oversized thin type. For Focus Design, that argues for removing small details and adding one or two big moments. Examples: a giant title that scales or locks into place on scroll, and a full-bleed scroll-through of client sites.
- The Cyd Stumpel SOTD shows that award-level excitement is possible with CSS-only techniques, with no WebGL and no heavy JS. That fits Focus Design's static Astro stack and its rule of no third-party requests.
- What excites in this category, as I infer from the tags: scale contrast (huge type next to small labels), full-bleed work showcases, scroll-linked reveals, horizontal scroll and page transitions. 3D, WebGL and sound are signatures of high-budget studios such as Immersive Garden. They would not fit a site selling €600-1,200 websites, and they would conflict with the speed promise Focus Design sells.

## 2. Apple product pages (iPhone, MacBook, AirPods): what creates the "wow", and how they keep restraint

### Takeaway
According to third-party teardowns and commentary, Apple's wow comes from three elements. The first is huge headlines with very short copy and lots of whitespace, one idea per screen. The second is a pinned ("sticky") stage that holds the product still while the content changes. The third is video or image sequences scrubbed by scroll, so the reader sets the pace. Restraint comes from one idea per section and whitespace as the main "decoration". The heavy scroll sequences are saved for a few signature moments, and the information itself is never hidden behind effects. I found no official Apple documentation; all of this is reconstruction.

### Cited Findings
- Core structure, from a third-party reconstruction of the MacBook Neo page: a tall scroll container provides scroll distance, and a sticky or pinned stage holds the product artwork in place while the content around it changes. — [ddingpy: Building an Apple-style scroll experience (MacBook Neo)](https://ddingpy.github.io/mynote/posts/building-an-apple-style-scroll-experience-like-the-macbook-neo-page) (seen only as a search summary)
- Scrubbing maps each scroll tick to a frame. It "demands video assets shot for scrubbing, not just watching". A common implementation draws an image sequence frame by frame onto a canvas, forwards and backwards. — [Autonoma Digital: cinematic scroll essay](https://autonoma-digital.pages.dev/blog/cinematic-scroll/); [21st.dev: scrubbable video reveal](https://21st.dev/@wensity/components/scrubbable-video-reveal.md)
- Recreations: Framer University and the Kirupa forum have rebuilt the Apple Vision Pro scroll effect with sticky positioning plus a scroll-scrub override. Framer Academy teaches the "zooming scroll effect like Apple.com". A 2015 post describes the original MacBook-page scrub. — [Framer University: Apple Vision Pro scroll animation](https://framer.university/resources/apple-vision-pro-scroll-animation); [Kirupa forum](https://forum.kirupa.com/t/recreating-apple-s-vision-pro-animation-in-css/681179); [Framer Academy: zoom scroll effect](https://framer.com/academy/lessons/zoom-scroll-effect); [jmeyers44 (2015)](https://jmeyers44.github.io/blog/2015/05/21/scrubbing-video-on-scroll)
- Modern alternative: CSS view timelines can keep animation and scroll in sync without JS. Use `animation-timeline: view()/scroll()` inside `@supports`, with an IntersectionObserver fallback. — [Builder.io: view timeline](https://www.builder.io/blog/view-timeline); [skills.sh: apple-design-web](https://www.skills.sh/s1gmamale1/apple-design-skills/apple-design-web)
- OPINION (commentary): "Headlines are very large, body copy is short, and generous whitespace slows the reader down and makes each statement feel deliberate". Each section carries one point: a short headline, a line or two of text and one large image. Whitespace is "the brand's primary chrome", with no decorative imagery filling negative space. — [Wensity: Anatomy of Apple's design language (2026)](https://ui.wensity.com/blog/the-anatomy-of-apples-design-language); [getdesign.md: Apple](https://getdesign.md/apple/design-md)
- OPINION: pinned scrubbing sequences suit products that justify 3-5 minutes of attention, not low-consideration purchases. The useful lesson from Apple is clarity, not copying its effects, and basic information should not be hidden behind scroll effects. — [DBS Website: How to design a website like Apple's](https://www.dbswebsite.com/blog/how-to-design-a-website-like-apples/); [Wensity](https://ui.wensity.com/blog/the-anatomy-of-apples-design-language)
- Cinematic scroll needs fallbacks "for reduced-motion, for slow connections, for the ten percent of your audience using a browser that doesn't quite support scroll-timeline". — [Autonoma Digital](https://autonoma-digital.pages.dev/blog/cinematic-scroll/)

### Inferences
- Focus Design already copies Apple's static layer: white, #1d1d1f, one blue, big tight titles. It lacks Apple's kinetic layer, which is the pinned stage plus scroll-scrubbed reveal. That is probably why the owner calls it "clean but without gas". The cheapest way to borrow that layer is one sticky section in which a client website mock-up (phone and desktop) fills the screen, changes or zooms on scroll, and swaps between real client projects. A canvas image sequence is not needed; CSS view timelines on screenshots are enough.
- Apple also uses colour moments (dark sections, product-colour backgrounds) between white sections. I saw this on Apple's pages but found no source for it in this session; see Gaps. One full-bleed dark or blue section with a huge sentence would be a low-cost "colour moment" that stays within the current palette.
- Apple's restraint rule, applied: one sentence per screen in the hero and in one or two "manifesto" sections only. Prices, process and contact stay plain, quick to scan and outside any pinned sequence.

### Gaps
- I found no official Apple statement or code-level teardown of the 2025-2026 iPhone 17 Pro, AirPods or MacBook pages. The search returned hardware reviews instead.
- I found no source documenting Apple's colour shifts and dark sections, or its typical section count and pacing.

## 3. What helps or hurts conversions and trust for a small-business service, and the performance and accessibility limits

### Takeaway
The evidence favours simple, familiar layouts at first glance and is negative on scrolljacking and autoplay video. NN/g's testing found scrolljacking threatens control, discoverability and task success, and it is tolerated only when it progressively reveals relevant information. NN/g advises scroll-triggered text effects only on secondary content, played once. Google-backed research found low visual complexity plus familiar layout is judged most appealing within 17-50 ms. Motion is safe if it stays on transform and opacity, avoids hijacking native scroll and respects reduced motion.

### Cited Findings
- EVIDENCE (NN/g usability testing): on the sites tested, scrolljacking threatened user control and freedom, discoverability, attention, efficiency and task success. On one page all participants became disoriented, and many tried to refresh or leave. Task-focused users have the least patience. Pages that changed scroll speed while requiring reading caused the worst problems. Scrolljacks were best tolerated when they progressively revealed relevant information, and disorientation was lower when fewer scroll actions were needed to change the screen. — [NN/g: Scrolljacking 101](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/scrolljacking-101/) (seen via search summary; full text blocked)
- EVIDENCE/GUIDELINE (NN/g, an older article, about 6 years old per search metadata): scroll-triggered text animations delay users. Use them for secondary, supporting content, not for the main body text. They should "only be activated the first time the user navigates down the page", and later views should show content immediately. They "should not unduly draw attention away from the content". — [NN/g: Scroll-Triggered Text Animations Delay Users](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/scroll-animations/)
- NN/g notes that parallax, carousel animations and scroll jacking can make users with vestibular disorders dizzy or nauseated and can trigger migraines. — [NN/g: Executing UX animations, duration and motion](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/animation-duration/)
- NN/g treats "scroll fading" (fade-in on scroll) as a separate pattern from scrolljacking. — [NN/g: Scroll fading 101](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/scroll-fading-101/)
- EVIDENCE (Tuch, Presslaber, Stöcklin, Opwis & Bargas-Avila, IJHCS 2012; covered by Google Research): websites with low visual complexity and high prototypicality (familiar layout) were rated most appealing. Visual complexity and prototypicality affect judgments from 17 ms of exposure, with the complexity effect appearing first. — [Google Research blog](https://research.google/blog/users-love-simple-and-familiar-designs-why-websites-need-to-make-a-great-first-impression/); [paper PDF](https://research.google.com/pubs/archive/38315.pdf)
- Mixed EVIDENCE on hero video, mostly vendor sources:
  - Unbounce reports one test where adding a video raised conversions 216%, with no methodology given, and it advises against autoplay as "an interruption technique that annoys people". — [Unbounce workshop: Testing your hero shot](https://workshops.unbounce.com/lesson/2-5-testing-your-hero-shot/)
  - An unverified "2,000 pages" study claims autoplay video heroes convert about 7% worse on average, with a median load of 2.4 s versus 1.3 s for image heroes. Its provenance is unclear, so treat it as weak. — [Digital Applied (2026)](https://www.digitalapplied.com/blog/landing-page-conversion-study-2000-pages-tested-2026)
  - A vertical-video test: a 10-second autoplay hero raised the play rate 40% but did not lift subscriptions, and it raised bounce on weak connections. — [compose.page](https://compose.page/a-b-test-ideas-for-vertical-video-landing-pages-that-increas)
  - Placement matters: video near the CTA beats video as decoration (opinion and case writing). — [Conversionlab](https://blog.conversionlab.no/insight/where-you-put-your-landing-page-video-decides-if-it-helps/)
- EVIDENCE (Chrome Lighthouse docs): non-composited animations, meaning those triggering style, layout or paint, are flagged and can increase CLS. Composited animations (transform and opacity) are excluded from CLS. — [Chrome for Developers: Avoid non-composited animations](https://developer.chrome.com/docs/lighthouse/performance/non-composited-animations)
- OPINION/analysis: smooth-scroll libraries such as Lenis intercept wheel and touch input and interpolate scroll in JS on the main thread. INP does not score scrolling, but main-thread animation work can delay the taps and clicks that INP measures. Scrolling can become a bottleneck on slow devices. Lenis exposes a `prefersReducedMotion` getter. — [Aidxn: Lenis vs native (2026)](https://aidxn.com/engineering/lenis-smooth-scroll-vs-native-2026/); [lenis on npm](https://www.npmjs.com/package/lenis)
- OPINION (critics versus defenders): some designers accept scrolljacking when it serves a purpose, while others call it an anti-pattern. NN/g is the only tested evidence among these sources. — [HubSpot: Scrolljacking, the good the bad the ugly](https://blog.hubspot.com/marketing/scrolljacking-the-good-the-bad-the-ugly); [Webdesigner Depot](https://webdesignerdepot.com/how-scrolljacking-breaks-ux-fundamentals/)
- Webflow's accessibility checklist notes that scrolljacking makes navigation harder for assistive-technology users. — [Webflow accessibility checklist: interactions](https://webflow.com/accessibility/checklist/category/interactions)

### Inferences
- For buyers like a renovation firm, a B&B or a gym owner, who are task-focused (what does it cost, what have you made, how do I contact you), the evidence argues for:
  - (a) excitement in the "showcase" zone (hero, portfolio, one manifesto section);
  - (b) plain, instantly readable pricing, process, FAQ and contact;
  - (c) native scroll, never hijacked;
  - (d) any reveal plays once and never hides prices or the CTA.
- The Google/Tuch finding supports keeping a familiar overall layout and low clutter. Removing small details (badges, micro-labels, many cards) and adding one big kinetic moment should raise perceived appeal, not lower it. This is my inference; the study tested static screenshots, not motion.
- Focus Design sells speed and quality. A heavy or janky "wow" would contradict the sales pitch, so Lighthouse and Core Web Vitals scores are part of the brand.
- Prefer autoplay-free or muted, short, poster-first loops, and only when the hero image is not the LCP bottleneck. A static or CSS-animated hero is the safer default.

### Gaps
- I found no Baymard or CXL study specifically on motion or animation for service businesses, and no 2023-2026 NN/g article on motion in marketing sites. The NN/g scroll-animation article is older than the 2023 window.
- I found no controlled study on whether "cool" studio sites raise conversion or trust for small-business buyers specifically.
- WCAG 2.3.3 "Animation from Interactions" (AAA) is the standard-level reference for disabling non-essential motion on interaction. I did not fetch it in this session: [W3C Understanding 2.3.3](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG21/Understanding/animation-from-interactions.html).

## 4. Cheap, performance-safe implementations in a static Astro site, and browser support in 2026

### Takeaway
By late 2026, CSS scroll-driven animations (`animation-timeline: scroll()/view()`) work in Chrome/Edge 115+, Safari 26 and Firefox 159. web.dev's October 2026 roundup counts them as Baseline Newly available. Same-document View Transitions have been Baseline since October 2025 (Firefox 144). Cross-document (MPA) view transitions, which is what Astro's static pages would use for page-to-page transitions, were still not supported in Firefox in the latest sources found. They degrade gracefully to a normal page load, though. GSAP with ScrollTrigger and SplitText became free for commercial use on 30 April 2025, but it is JS weight that the CSS route mostly removes.

### Cited Findings
- Scroll-driven animations are listed with Chrome/Edge 115+, Safari 26 and Firefox 159 (first version enabled by default), and the feature is now Baseline Newly available. — [web.dev: New to the web platform in October 2026](https://web.dev/blog/web-platform-10-2026) (via search summary). Older sources conflict: Caniuse showed no Firefox support through v155, and a dev guide said behind a flag in early 2026. — [Caniuse: scroll-driven animations](https://caniuse.com/wf-scroll-driven-animations); [web-features explorer](https://web-platform-dx.github.io/web-features-explorer/features/scroll-driven-animations)
- WebKit published "A guide to scroll-driven animations with just CSS" (Safari 26 beta), and scroll-driven animations are an Interop 2026 focus area. — [WebKit guide](https://webkit.org/?p=17101); [WebKit: Announcing Interop 2026](https://webkit.org/blog/17818/announcing-interop-2026)
- A W3C TAG design review, opened November 2025, covers a new "Scroll-Triggered Animations" spec: animations triggered at a scroll point that then play on time, unlike scrubbed animations. It is not shipped guidance. — [W3C TAG #1167](https://tag-github-bot.w3.org/gh/w3ctag/design-reviews/1167)
- Same-document View Transitions became Baseline Newly available when Firefox 144 shipped them (published 16 Oct 2025). Firefox 144 did not include view-transition types. — [web.dev: same-document view transitions are Baseline](https://web.dev/blog/same-document-view-transitions-are-now-baseline-newly-available); [web.dev Oct 2025](https://web.dev/blog/web-platform-10-2025)
- Cross-document View Transitions: Baseline blocked by Firefox since December 2024. Mozilla tracks it in bug 1860854, and Caniuse lists Firefox as unsupported. These sources are from late 2025; I found no 2026 update. — [web-features explorer: cross-document view transitions](https://web-platform-dx.github.io/web-features-explorer/features/cross-document-view-transitions); [Bugzilla 2001878](https://bugzilla.mozilla.org/show_bug.cgi?id=2001878); [Caniuse](https://caniuse.com/cross-document-view-transitions); [Chrome: view transitions in 2025](https://developer.chrome.com/blog/view-transitions-in-2025)
- GSAP: Webflow made GSAP "100% free", including the former Club plugins (ScrollTrigger, SplitText, Flip, MorphSVG and others), for commercial use, effective 30 April 2025. The free licence still restricts using it in tools that compete with Webflow's visual builder, which does not matter for a client site. — [Webflow blog: GSAP becomes free](https://webflow.com/blog/gsap-becomes-free); [GSAP standard licence](https://gsap.com/community/standard-license/); [GSAP GitHub](https://github.com/greensock/GSAP)
- Recommended pattern: `animation-timeline` inside `@supports`, with an IntersectionObserver fallback or a static fallback. Animate only transform and opacity, and respect `prefers-reduced-motion`. — [skills.sh apple-design-web](https://www.skills.sh/s1gmamale1/apple-design-skills/apple-design-web); [Chrome Lighthouse docs](https://developer.chrome.com/docs/lighthouse/performance/non-composited-animations)
- Cyd Stumpel's 2025 SOTD portfolio proves the CSS-only route (View Transitions + Scroll-Driven Animations) is award-grade. — [Awwwards](https://www.awwwards.com/sites/cyd-stumpel-portfolio-2025)

### Inferences
These implementation ideas fit the project rules in CLAUDE.md: transform and opacity only, inside `@media (prefers-reduced-motion: no-preference)`, no third-party requests, and tokens only. They are not tested here.
1. **Kinetic hero title, CSS only.** The giant title starts as one oversized line and, on scroll, scales down and translates into place using `animation-timeline: scroll()`, with the hero's own scroll range as the range. The static fallback is the final state, so nothing is hidden for older browsers.
2. **Pinned "work" stage.** A tall section with a `position: sticky` frame. Client site screenshots (Astro `<Picture>`, lazy) stack and fade or scale in with `view()` timelines, Apple-style, with no canvas sequence. Keep it short, 3-4 projects and about 1-1.5 screen-heights each, given NN/g's finding that fewer scroll actions per change means less disorientation.
3. **One colour moment.** A full-bleed `#1d1d1f` or blue section with one huge sentence ("one sentence per screen"). The only motion is an opacity and translate reveal, played once. Add the needed values to tokens.css first.
4. **Page transitions.** Astro's view-transition support or native `@view-transition { navigation: auto; }` for project-to-detail morphs. Firefox users get a normal navigation, so the cost of the progressive enhancement is near zero.
5. **Hover and micro-interactions only on the work cards.** Image scale on hover/focus. Keep the native cursor: jurors penalise hidden cursors, and touch users never see cursor effects anyway.
6. **Avoid:** Lenis or other smooth-scroll hijacking, autoplay hero video, WebGL/3D and sound. Each of these costs speed, INP or accessibility, which are what Focus Design sells.
7. **GSAP + ScrollTrigger only if** a sequence needs timeline control that CSS cannot express. It is free now, but it adds JS. Load it only on the page that needs it.

### Gaps
- I could not confirm cross-document View Transitions status in Firefox as of November 2026; the latest sources are from late 2025.
- Firefox 159's release date and exact scroll-driven-animation scope (for example, whether `view()` with named ranges is fully supported) are unverified because web.dev could not be fetched.
- I found no measured data on how much CSS scroll-driven animation affects Core Web Vitals compared with GSAP ScrollTrigger on mid-range phones.
- Codrops and Smashing Magazine 2024-2026 tutorials were not reached in this session.
