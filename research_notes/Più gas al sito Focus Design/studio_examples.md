## 1. Which studio/freelancer sites feel premium and exciting, and why (specific devices)?

### Takeaway
The sites that stand out pair one bold, memorable device (a founder-led offer, one signature interaction, or one giant typographic/name moment) with an extremely simple offer structure. Productized studios (Designjoy, Goodspeed, Zero-Meeting) get their "premium" from confident constraint: one price, one deliverable, one deadline, one CTA. Portfolio-grade freelancer sites (Snellenberg, Bruno Simon, Cuberto) get their "exciting" from a single, well-made signature interaction, not from many effects.

### Cited Findings
**Productized studios (closest to Focus Design's model)**
- Designjoy has run since 2017 and the homepage states the business "is run entirely by Brett", so the founder is the brand. — [designjoy.co (search snippet)](https://designjoy.co/)
- Designjoy's top messaging is a single tagline about one subscription for all design needs, with only two CTAs: see pricing, or book an intro call. One version reads "Book a 15-min intro call". — [designjoy.co](https://designjoy.co/); [designjoy.co (indexed copy)](https://www.designjoy.co/)
- Designjoy copy leans on removing risk and friction: "scale up or down as needed, and pause or cancel at anytime", plus an average delivery of about two business days. — [designjoy.co](https://www.designjoy.co/)
- Designjoy's page follows a short, standard order: How it works → Benefits → Services → Pricing → FAQs. (Seen on a third-party Framer recreation, not the original.) — [DesignJoy Framer recreation](https://designjoywebsite.framer.website/)
- Lapa Ninja lists the Designjoy landing page (published 2021) as "A design agency with a twist". — [Lapa Ninja](https://www.lapa.ninja/post/designjoy/)
- Memberstack's case study frames Designjoy as a productized service: fixed price, fixed expectations. — [Memberstack showcase](https://www.memberstack.com/showcase/how-i-built-designjoy)
- Designjoy's price is reported at $4,995/month (Ycode, clone sites) and at $5,995/month (another roundup). The sources conflict and the price has probably changed over time. — [Ycode guide](https://www.ycode.com/blog/a-complete-guide-to-subscription-web-design-agencies); [we.inc / roundup snippet](https://we.inc/blog/productized-web-design); [1capture review](https://www.1capture.io/blog/designjoy-review)
- The Designjoy layout is cloned again and again on Webflow and Framer (designjoyclone.webflow.io, copyofdesignjoy.webflow.io, designjoybyali.webflow.io), which shows how influential the format is in the productized-design niche. — [designjoyclone.webflow.io](https://designjoyclone.webflow.io/); [copyofdesignjoy.webflow.io](https://copyofdesignjoy.webflow.io/)
- Goodspeed (London, founded 2022) sells a landing-page product "live in five working days", from $2,500 fixed. Design, build, forms, tracking and two rounds of revisions are included. A named, bounded deliverable with a deadline in the product name. — [Goodspeed product page](https://goodspeed.studio/product/landing-page); [goodspeed.studio](https://goodspeed.studio/)
- Zero-Meeting Studio's whole proposition is its name: a $499 one-page landing page, delivered async in 48–72 h, no calls. — [zeromeeting.site (search snippet)](https://www.zeromeeting.site/)
- Lander Studio: landing pages from $3,499 in about 2 weeks (Framer/Webflow/Next.js), per third-party comparisons. — [Hexcode directory](https://www.hexcode.design/directory/landing-page-design-studios-for-startups); [Deserve Studio blog](https://deserve.studio/blog/best-landing-page-design-agencies-for-startups)

- Bruno Simon's portfolio is a driveable 3D car world (Three.js/WebGL, keyboard/touch/gamepad) that holds the work and links. ### Inferences
- Two families, two lessons. (a) Productized studios such as Designjoy, Goodspeed and Zero-Meeting look "premium" because they are radically simple and sure of themselves: the offer reads like a product (name, price, deadline). (b) Award portfolios look "exciting" because of one signature interaction (Snellenberg's magnetic buttons, Bruno Simon's car, Cuberto's cursor), not many small effects. For Focus Design, the best fit is family (a)'s structure with one carefully made family-(b) moment.
- Snellenberg and Cuberto rely on cursor/hover effects, which do not exist on mobile. Small-business owners in Milan will mostly see the site on a phone, so the signature moment must also work on touch and scroll (and, per CLAUDE.md, use only transform/opacity inside `prefers-reduced-motion: no-preference`).
- Bruno Simon-style WebGL is too heavy for a 600–1,200 € studio and would contradict the "no third-party requests, fast static site" rules. It is a reference for the *principle* (one memorable idea), not the technique.

### Gaps
- I could not open any page, so hero typography (typefaces, sizes), colours and actual motion were not verified first-hand for any site. Designjoy's typeface/colour, exact current headline and current price need a browser check.
- No reliable source described Designjoy's founder photo treatment or its portfolio marquee. Commonly recalled from memory, but not verified here.
- No Italian productized studio at the 600–1,200 € level was found in the search results. Italian studios found were award-driven agencies, not direct competitors.
- Typewolf, Siteinspire, Land-book and One Page Love specific entries were not retrievable through search snippets.

## 2. How do the best ones show their portfolio?

### Takeaway
The evidence I could find shows three patterns: (1) productized studios show little work, often as a quick strip or marquee, because the *offer* is the hero. (2) Fixed-price studios use short written mini-case studies with screenshots and a measurable result. (3) Award portfolios turn browsing the work into the interaction itself: a hover gallery, a cursor that previews the project image, or a 3D world. I found no sourced example of live iframes on studio sites.

### Cited Findings
- Goodspeed's showcase content is a write-up of three startup landing pages (LinQ, Assemble, Crayo AI) with screenshots plus the layout, copy and CTA reasoning behind each. That is portfolio as explained decisions, not a gallery. — [Goodspeed Framer examples](https://goodspeed.studio/framer-landing-page-examples/startup)
- Goodspeed's Reka case study uses a one-line outcome format: "three solution pages in a week" and "100% on Lighthouse SEO" (self-reported). — [goodspeed.studio](https://goodspeed.studio/)
- The "project gallery with mouse hover" pattern (a project list where hovering shows a floating preview image that follows the cursor) is so associated with Awwwards-style portfolios that it is taught as "Build an Awwwards Project Gallery" in Next.js/GSAP tutorials modelled on Snellenberg-type sites. — [Olivier Larose tutorial](https://blog.olivierlarose.com/tutorials/project-gallery-mouse-hover); [Olivier Larose landing tutorial](https://blog.olivierlarose.com/tutorials/awwwards-landing-page)
- Cuberto's cursor can hold images and video, so project previews appear inside the cursor on hover. — [Cuberto LinkedIn](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/cuberto_cuberto-mouse-follower-open-source-activity-6920004418801188864-4rez)
- Bruno Simon places projects as objects you drive to inside the 3D world. — [Creative Bloq](https://www.creativebloq.com/news/3d-car-portfolio)
- The One Page Love portfolio category has 3,700+ real examples with full-length screenshots, a useful pool for comparing portfolio display. — [UI Goodies](https://uigoodies.com/resources/onepagelove-portfolios)
- "Free homepage preview" competitors mostly show work as static mockups. AIERA Studio offers "a private preview of your new homepage, with no cost and no obligation", and Motivent stresses that the mockup is "a true design made for your business, not a stock template". — [AIERA Studio](https://aierastudioco.com/); [Motivent offer](https://motiventmarketing.com/offers/website-redesign-home-services/)

### Inferences
- For Focus Design, the free-72-hour-preview offer *is* the portfolio device. A before/after of a real client (old site vs Focus Design preview vs final), or a short scroll story "giorno 0 → 72 ore → online", would show the work and explain the offer at the same time. This follows from the Goodspeed "explained decisions + one metric" pattern and the competitors' "see it before you pay" framing.
- A hover-preview project list works on desktop only. On mobile, it is better as large full-bleed screenshots (`<Picture>` avif/webp, lazy) in a horizontal swipe or a simple stack.
- Live iframes of client sites would add third-party requests and weight, against the project rules. Static screenshots (optionally short muted self-hosted video loops) are the safer choice.

### Gaps
- No sourced example of a small studio using live iframes or device mockups as its main portfolio display. I could not verify Designjoy's work strip/marquee.
- No data found comparing which portfolio format converts better for small-business buyers.

## 3. What makes a service-business site feel "alive" while staying simple?

### Takeaway
"Alive" comes from three things: one signature interaction made with care (magnetic buttons, an animated name, a cursor), a visible real person (founder photo and name in or near the hero), and copy with a clear voice that removes friction ("pause or cancel anytime", "no meetings"). Everything else stays plain.

### Cited Findings
- Snellenberg's hero is the clearest "alive but simple" case: animated name plus one photo of him, matched colours, and magnetic buttons as the only constant micro-interaction. — [Medium roundup](https://halimshams.medium.com/5-portfolio-websites-that-will-blow-up-your-mind-3f375db2741e); [Envato Tuts+ Motion course](https://webdesign.tutsplus.com/motion-design-for-the-web-free-course--ytc-69c)
- Designjoy's founder-run positioning ("run entirely by Brett") plus a 15-minute call with the owner makes a one-person service feel personal rather than corporate. — [designjoy.co](https://designjoy.co/); [1capture review](https://www.1capture.io/blog/designjoy-review)
- A short founder's note with photo and title on the homepage helps web-design clients see who they will work with. Share the start of the story, then link to the About page. — [JoshHall.co](https://joshhall.co/add-a-founders-note-to-your-homepage-to-get-more-web-design-clients/)
- Bruno Simon's motivation for his signature idea was physics in web interaction, chosen in a form (a car) that stays easy to navigate. — [Creative Bloq](https://www.creativebloq.com/news/3d-car-portfolio)

### Inferences
- For Focus Design: replace many small details with ONE signature moment tied to the name (see Q5, the "focus" pull from blur to sharp), a real photo of Lorenzo/the team, and a single repeated CTA.
- The signature moment must be cheap to build in plain CSS (transform/opacity only, reduced-motion-safe) and must work on mobile scroll, not only hover.

### Gaps
- No quantitative evidence found (A/B tests) that a signature interaction lifts conversion for service businesses. The case rests on memorability and award recognition, not measured leads.

## 4. What do they deliberately leave out (reduction)?

### Takeaway
The productized sites leave out custom quotes, calls and meetings (or shrink them to 15 minutes), multi-tier menus, long service lists and extra pages. One offer, one price, one deadline and one or two CTAs is the recurring pattern.

### Cited Findings
- Designjoy: only two CTAs (see pricing / book a 15-minute call), a single standard plan in many versions, and a short section order. — [designjoy.co](https://www.designjoy.co/); [Framer recreation](https://designjoywebsite.framer.website/)
- Zero-Meeting Studio removes meetings entirely. The constraint is the brand. — [zeromeeting.site](https://www.zeromeeting.site/)
- Goodspeed bundles everything (design, build, forms, tracking, two revision rounds) into one fixed price and turnaround, so it needs no à-la-carte pricing. — [Goodspeed product page](https://goodspeed.studio/product/landing-page)
- Free-mockup competitors often hide the real price or attach a monthly plan, e.g. "free with a monthly plan from $300" (CR Local Business) or "$0 upfront, $149/month" (Double G). Others are transparent: First & Forward lists "free homepage mockup first, $1,000 for a complete site", and Designgratuit says the homepage "is yours to keep, whoever builds it". — [CR Local Business](https://crlocalbusiness.com/free-website-mockup); [Double G Consulting](https://www.double-g-consulting.com/website-design.html); [First & Forward](https://firstandforwardmarketing.com/); [Designgratuit](https://designgratuit.com/)

### Inferences
- Focus Design's 600–1,200 € range with a free 72-hour preview is already a productized offer. Its advantage is stating price and deadline openly where many free-mockup competitors hide them. Reduction means: one hero promise ("Vedi la tua homepage in 72 ore, gratis"), one CTA repeated, fewer badges and micro-details.
- "Who keeps the preview if I say no" is a trust point that competitors answer explicitly (Designgratuit). Focus Design should answer it in one line. Policy to be confirmed by the client: [TODO: policy sull'anteprima se il cliente non procede].

## 5. (Added brief) How do small studios and solo founders give their site a personality of its own?

### Takeaway
Personality on credible small-studio sites comes from (1) a recognisable person (photo, name, short story), (2) a voice that speaks the client's worry in their words and removes friction, and (3) one recurring idea tied to the brand name. Humour works best in microcopy (CTAs, forms, error states), not in the headline. Small-business clients are reassured by a real person, plain language and concrete promises. Effects that make the site harder to use (custom cursors, games) read as gimmicks to them.

### Cited Findings
**Voice and microcopy (named examples)**
- Elizabeth McCravy (website designer) writes CTAs from the ideal client's point of view instead of "Learn More", which the author argues gets more clicks. — [Sarah Klongerbo](https://sarahklongerbo.com/blog/microcopy-in-writing/)
- Wandering Aimfully's unsubscribe link is cited as "empathetic, direct, and even a little funny", an example of personality placed in a low-stakes spot. — [Sarah Klongerbo](https://sarahklongerbo.com/blog/microcopy-in-writing/)
- GitHub's contact page line "If you just want to say hi, that's cool too" is a classic warm microcopy example. — [Designmodo](https://designmodo.com/microcopy/)
- Microcopy is "the voice of the product, speaking directly to the user in moments where clarity or encouragement is needed most". — [Justinmind](https://www.justinmind.com/blog/microcopy-improve-ux-with-these-10-examples/)
- Awwwards keeps a collection dedicated to microcopy and UX writing on studio sites. — [Awwwards collection](https://www.awwwards.com/awwwards/collections/microcopy-and-ux-writing/); [Awwwards article](https://www.awwwards.com/top-examples-of-ux-writing-and-microcopy-writing-as-a-design-tool.html)
- Friction-removing voice as personality: Designjoy ("pause or cancel at anytime") and Zero-Meeting (the name itself promises no calls). — [designjoy.co](https://www.designjoy.co/); [zeromeeting.site](https://www.zeromeeting.site/)

**Founder presence**
- Founder note with photo and title on the homepage, short story, link to About. Cited as a way to win web-design clients. — [JoshHall.co](https://joshhall.co/add-a-founders-note-to-your-homepage-to-get-more-web-design-clients/)
- Designjoy: "run entirely by Brett", intro call with the owner. — [designjoy.co](https://designjoy.co/); [1capture](https://www.1capture.io/blog/designjoy-review)
- Snellenberg: own photo plus animated name as the hero. — [Medium roundup](https://halimshams.medium.com/5-portfolio-websites-that-will-blow-up-your-mind-3f375db2741e)

**Signature idea tied to the name**
- Zero-Meeting Studio: the name, the offer and the process are the same idea (async, no calls). — [zeromeeting.site](https://www.zeromeeting.site/)
- Designjoy ("joy"), described as "a design agency with a twist". — [Lapa Ninja](https://www.lapa.ninja/post/designjoy/)
- Bruno Simon: a signature idea tied to his craft (physics, play). — [Creative Bloq](https://www.creativebloq.com/news/3d-car-portfolio)

### Inferences
- **Focus/lens idea for "Focus Design" (proposal, not sourced practice):** one recurring motif. (a) Hero: the client's site preview starts blurred/out of focus and snaps sharp on load or scroll ("da sfocato a nitido"). Done with opacity crossfade of two images, or a scale tweak; a CSS `filter: blur()` animation would break the transform/opacity-only rule unless the rule is reviewed. (b) A focus-ring/viewfinder corner marks (like a camera AF frame) as the recurring graphic element around the key CTA and project screenshots. (c) Copy that plays on it sparingly: "Mettiamo a fuoco la tua attività", "Il tuo sito, a fuoco in 72 ore". (d) The 72-hour preview framed as "il primo scatto". One motif used in 3–4 places beats ten micro-details.
- **Builds trust for small-business clients:** a real founder photo and name (Lorenzo, with Paolo if they want), plain Italian copy, a visible price range and deadline, a clear statement of what happens if they say no to the preview, real client before/after shots, and a Milan location. All of these match the "see it before you pay" competitor messaging and the founder-note advice above.
- **Risks feeling gimmicky:** custom cursors and magnetic buttons (desktop-only, irrelevant for phone-first local clients), 3D/WebGL, heavy page transitions, puns in every heading, and stock "creative" humour. Humour belongs in microcopy (form confirmation, 404, the button under the price), not in the main promise.
- Rules to respect: all personality copy must be in Italian, and no client facts may be invented (e.g. no made-up founder story, years in business or number of clients): [TODO: breve storia del fondatore, foto reale di Lorenzo/Paolo, numero reale di siti consegnati].

### Gaps
- No sourced examples of Italian solo designers with distinctive microcopy were found. Searches returned only English-language examples.
- No study found on which personality devices raise trust or conversion for small-business buyers. The trust/gimmick split above is reasoned from the sources, not measured.
- Handwritten or personal touches (signatures, notes, scribbles) on studio sites: no sourced examples found in this session.
