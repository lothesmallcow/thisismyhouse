# What drives bookings/trials on independent fitness studio websites (Milan focus, 2024-2026)

Method note: page fetches were blocked for most hosts in this session (goteamup.com, glofox.com, brightlocal.com, fostermag.com, corework.net failed to resolve). Most figures below come from search-result summaries of the cited pages, not from reading the full page. Treat exact wording as "to verify" before publishing.

## 1. Website factors linked to bookings/trials (evidence per rubric check)

### Takeaway
Hard, independent evidence that ties single website features to bookings is thin. Most numbers are vendor claims (booking-software firms) or US consumer surveys. The strongest support is for: online self-booking, a free trial/intro offer, Google reviews and star rating, and mobile-first design.

### Cited Findings
- **Free trial / intro offer.** TeamUp (vendor, UK) says it processed 34M+ class bookings in 2025, and that gyms that switched on free trials saw 3.5x more bookings than gyms that did not (VENDOR CLAIM; correlation, not causation) — [TeamUp 2026 Group Fitness Trend Report](https://goteamup.com/resources/introducing-the-2026-group-fitness-industry-trend-report); [Athletech News](https://athletechnews.com/what-34-million-bookings-reveal-about-retention-in-fitness/)
- Mindbody advises pricing intro offers below drop-in prices, for example a first month at 30-50% off (VENDOR ADVICE, no outcome data) — [Mindbody blog](https://www.mindbodyonline.com/en-gb/business/education/blog/how-to-intro-an-offer-they-cant-refuse)
- Mindbody says fitness customers average 44% more bookings 6 months after joining, and its app drives 22,000+ first purchases a month. Mindbody itself says the figures are "not guaranteed" (VENDOR CLAIM) — [Mindbody fitness page](https://www.mindbodyonline.com/en-gb/business/fitness?landing=y)
- **Online self-booking vs "contact us".** A 2024 survey cited by Shopify says 81% of clients prefer to book directly with the provider on their own (secondary source; the original survey was not identified) — [Shopify](https://www.shopify.com/blog/fitness-studio-scheduling-software). SchedulingKit's 2026 roundup gives 82%, from a self-selected European sample — [SchedulingKit](https://schedulingkit.com/hub/industry-guides/fitness-booking-statistics)
- **Mobile use of the booking widget.** Glofox (vendor) says 53.4% of users reached its website booking integration from a phone. Another Glofox figure gives 55.79% mobile vs 44.21% desktop. Glofox also notes that mobile traffic may convert less well than desktop. No date shown (VENDOR) — [Glofox blog](https://glofox.com/blog/how-glofoxs-unique-data-insights-can-increase-your-booking-conversions)
- **Google reviews.** BrightLocal LCRS 2025 (US): 83% of consumers use Google for local reviews, and 74% use at least two review platforms — [BrightLocal LCRS 2025](https://www.brightlocal.com/research/local-consumer-review-survey-2025/); [Search Lab Digital summary](https://searchlabdigital.com/blog/data-from-brightlocals-consumer-review-survey/)
- BrightLocal LCRS 2026 (US, 1,002 adults, published Feb 2026): 68% require at least 4 stars (up from 55%), 31% only use businesses rated 4.5+, and Google's share of review reading fell to 71%. All figures come from secondary summaries — [PinMeTo summary](https://www.pinmeto.com/news/brightlocal-local-consumer-review-survey-2026/); [BrightLocal LCRS](https://www.brightlocal.com/research/local-consumer-review-survey/). A widely repeated figure that "92% need 4 stars" is attributed to Chatmeter, not BrightLocal, so it is unverified — [ClickMinded](https://www.clickminded.com/online-review-statistics/)
- **Gymdesk** (vendor) has a 2026 roundup of gym website examples, "what converts, what doesn't" (opinion/qualitative) — [Gymdesk](https://gymdesk.com/blog/9-gym-website-ideas-what-makes-a-good-gym-website)
- In Milan, Wellhub's listing for Ashtanga Yoga Italia says "booking is required to take part," so booking is standard even at small studios — [Wellhub](https://wellhub.com/it-it/search/partners/ashtanga-yoga-italia/)
- Free first lessons are common among Milan freelance instructors ("1a lezione offerta" on many Superprof profiles), and PT listings offer free first consultations — [Superprof pilates Milano](https://www.superprof.it/lezione/pilates/milano/principiante/); [Superprof PT](https://www.superprof.it/personal-trainer-certificato-project-invictus-milano-coaching-privato-misura-colloquio-conoscitivo-gratuito.html)

### Inferences
- Rubric checks with the best evidence: online booking (not just a form), a visible trial/intro offer, Google rating/reviews shown or linked, and a mobile-first layout.
- Checks with no direct evidence found, but supported by general UX logic and the CLAUDE.md conventions: number of taps to book, booking button visible without scrolling, schedule visible, prices visible, instructor bios, map link, WhatsApp/tap-to-call. Score them as good practice, not as proven drivers.
- In Milan, a free or cheap first lesson is a local norm, so a missing intro offer is a competitive gap.

### Gaps
- I found no independent study (Think with Google, academic, BrightLocal) measuring the effect on fitness bookings of tap count, above-the-fold button, published prices, published schedule, or instructor bios.
- Momence, Arketa, WellnessLiving and Zen Planner turned up no usable statistics. The Mindbody consumer-report figures I found were vendor infographics with jumbled labels.
- No fitness-specific data on page speed vs conversion.

## 2. Mobile share and "near me" behaviour

### Takeaway
Most fitness website traffic is mobile (about 55-75% depending on source). "Near me" searches are mobile-heavy, and gyms are a top "near me" category. The Google figures are old (2014-2017) and US-centric.

### Cited Findings
- CuFinder 2026 fitness benchmark: mobile 72.4%, desktop 25.1%, tablet 2.5% — [CuFinder](https://cufinder.io/blog/benchmarks/fitness-and-wellness/). Other aggregators give 70%+ or 76%+ — [Ranktracker](https://www.ranktracker.com/blog/fitness-and-gym-seo-statistics-for-2024); [Webtonic](https://www.webtonic.io/blog/fitness-technical-seo-statistics)
- Contrary data: a UK benchmark for Q1 2024 found health and fitness clubs got more desktop than mobile traffic (UK clubs, not boutiques) — [ClickThrough Marketing](https://clickthrough-marketing.com/industry-reports/sector-insights/uk-health-and-fitness-clubs-digital-marketing-benchmark-report-q1-2024)
- Think with Google lists gyms among the most popular "near me" searches. Interest in "near me" grew 34x since 2011, and 80% of these searches were on mobile in Q4 2014 (OLD, US) — [Think with Google](https://thinkwithgoogle.com/consumer-insights/near-me-searches/)
- Mobile searches for "near me now" grew 150%+, and for "near me today/tonight" 900%+, over 2015-2017 (OLD, US, all categories) — [Think with Google local search stats](https://thinkwithgoogle.com/data/local-search-statistics)
- A third-party Google Trends analysis (Jan 2025) says "near me" searches reached all-time highs in 2023-2024 — [The Media Captain](https://www.themediacaptain.com/incredible-near-me-google-search-statistics/)

### Inferences
- Weighting mobile checks heavily (button above the fold on 390 px, tap-to-call, speed) fits the traffic data, even though direct booking-conversion data is missing.

### Gaps
- No Italy- or Milan-specific mobile share or "palestra vicino a me" data was found. Google Trends IT could be checked manually.

### Inferences
- Published prices are common among Milan reformer studios: 6 of the sites found show price lists. A scorer can treat "prices visible" as normal and their absence as a minus.

### Gaps

- No Milan-specific market size. Italy club revenue should be verified in the EHFMR (paywalled).
- ClassPass and Gymdesk adoption in Milan not found.

## 4. Things to avoid: medical claims, health data, certificato medico

### Takeaway
Health data (including medical certificates) is a special category under Art. 9 GDPR. Website forms should not collect it. Whether a certificato medico is required depends on the studio's legal form, so a site should not state a legal rule without checking.

### Cited Findings
- Health data is "special category" data under Art. 9 GDPR. The Garante fined a medical practice €4,000 for telling a gym about a patient's suspended fitness for sport without her consent — [Diritto.it](https://www.diritto.it/?p=194335); [Garante](https://garanteprivacy.it/garante/document?ID=9109364)
- TeamSystem guidance covers GDPR, medical certificates and the DPO (Art. 37 DPO duty for large-scale health data) in sports centres — [TeamSystem](https://www.teamsystem.com/magazine/sport-e-wellness/gdpr-certificati-medici-dpo-centro-sportivo/)
- **Certificato medico:** for members of ASD/SSD clubs or federation-affiliated bodies, a non-competitive certificate is usually required (Decreto Balduzzi). Commercial gyms count as "ludico-motoria" activity, where a certificate is not mandatory, though many gyms ask for one anyway — [Altroconsumo](https://www.altroconsumo.it/salute/dal-medico/news/certificato-medico); [Lentepubblica](https://www.lentepubblica.it/cittadini-e-imprese/certificato-medico-palestra/); [My-personaltrainer](https://www.my-personaltrainer.it/sport/chi-puo-fare-il-certificato-medico-per-la-palestra-e-per-l-attivita-agonistica.html)

### Inferences
- Scorer red flags: a contact or trial form asking about pathologies, injuries or pregnancy; medical or therapeutic claims ("cura il mal di schiena") made by non-medical staff; stated certificate rules with no source.

### Gaps
- No Italian source specific to medical or health claims on fitness websites (AGCM or Codice del Consumo cases) was found in this pass.
