## Q1. Productized design and web services, and studios that sell: section order and CTA placement

### Takeaway
Productized design sites follow one dominant skeleton: hero with an offer and a risk reverser ("pause or cancel anytime"), a work marquee, a 3-step process, benefits, pricing with a fixed price on the page, FAQ, then a final CTA. Small Italian web designers who serve PMI mostly hide prices behind "preventivo". The few who publish a starting price (for example DSI Design at €1,500+IVA) stand out, which suggests price transparency is a gap a Milan studio can exploit.

## Q2. SaaS and product pages with 3D or interactive product shots: placement, performance, and competition with the CTA

### Takeaway
On the pages known for this, the 3D or motion is either the product itself shown at large size with the CTA in persistent chrome (Apple) or placed beside or below the hero text (Stripe's globe further down, Linear's static-then-animated screenshot). The engineering pattern is consistent: a static poster first, lazy-loaded scene, a still frame under reduced motion, a fallback when WebGL is off, a DPR cap, and pausing rendering off-screen.

### Cited Findings
- Apple's scroll animations (AirPods Pro, MacBook Pro) are not `<video>`. A `<canvas>` draws pre-extracted frames, typically **60–180 images**, loaded progressively, with each scroll position mapped to a frame index. — [mejba.me (Spanish)](https://www.mejba.me/es/blog/3d-scroll-animations-ai-claude-code); [CSS-Tricks](https://css-tricks.com/?p=308477); [DEV tutorial](https://dev.to/bekbrace/creating-a-scrolling-animation-for-apple-airpods-1g0m)
- Swapping `img.src` on scroll is slow. Use a sprite sheet or preloaded show/hide frames instead. In one GSAP forum case, converting PNG to WebP cut a hero from about 21MB to about 4MB. — [GSAP forum](https://gsap.com/community/forums/topic/25188-airpods-image-sequence-animation-using-scrolltrigger/)
- Stripe built its homepage globe with Three.js rather than a custom engine. It is a 1:40M-scale earth in three layers: a semi-transparent ocean sphere, a sphere of twinkling dots, and animated arcs. GitHub published a similar dot-globe write-up. — [Stripe blog](https://stripe.com/blog/globe); [GitHub blog](https://github.blog/engineering/engineering-principles/how-we-built-the-github-globe/)
- Spline's own docs recommend **no more than 3 embeds per page** and using `<spline-viewer>` over iframes, because the viewer supports lazy loading. — [Spline docs](https://docs.spline.design/doc/how-to-optimize-your-scene/doczPMIye7Ko)
- Spline scenes that load on first render affect **LCP and TBT**. Lazy-load any 3D that is not the first thing on screen, using `React.lazy` + `Suspense` in React. — [Hedrick Spline guide 2026](https://hedrick.io/post/spline-3d-guide); [21st.dev](https://21st.dev/blog/spline-3d-scenes-react)
- Show a placeholder image until the scene loads and a static image on slow devices. A GIF or MP4 often does the job with less load. — [Jack Redley](https://jackredley.design/articles/optimizing-spline-3d-files-for-fast-loading-websites-in-webflow-and-framer); [Framer community thread](https://www.framer.community/c/support/spline-3d-models-slows-website-on-some-pcs)
- Anecdotal budgets: ambient shadows are a "huge FPS killer", and above about **20k polygons** (including lights and shadows) FPS drops on weak GPUs. — [Framer community](https://www.framer.community/c/support/spline-3d-models-slows-website-on-some-pcs)
- Under `prefers-reduced-motion: reduce`, render a still frame with no requestAnimationFrame loop, or slow the updates. Provide a fallback for weak devices or disabled WebGL so users never see a blank canvas. Cap `renderer.setPixelRatio(Math.min(devicePixelRatio, 2))` and pause rendering when the hero is off-screen. — [MengTo webgl-landing-steering skill](https://www.ui-skills.com/skills/mengto/webgl-landing-steering/); [MengTo three.js skill](https://skills.sh/mengto/skills/threejs); [21st.dev Prism Hero](https://21st.dev/@bevelui/components/prism-hero.md)
- Never put `loading="lazy"` on the hero `<img>`, described as "the single most common way to wreck LCP". The DOM should be the default, with canvas or WebGL used only where needed. — [claudskills enhance-web-web3d](https://claudskills.com/skills/enhance-web-web3d/SKILL.md)
- Product screenshots appeared in 9% (42/488) and video in 14% (68/488) of tested hero experiments in one 2026 dataset. These count what was tested, not what is common. — [Lazyweb Labs](https://labs.lazyweb.com/research/can-i-measure-hero-media-mix-from-this-data.md)
- Lapa Ninja keeps a dedicated "3D websites" category with 156 examples to reference. — [Lapa Ninja 3D](https://www.lapa.ninja/category/3d-websites/)

### Inferences
- **LCP nuance, from prior knowledge of web.dev docs and not re-fetched:** `<canvas>` is not an LCP candidate. Only img, image inside svg, a video poster or first frame, CSS `url()` backgrounds and block text count. With a WebGL-only hero, LCP falls to the headline text, which is usually fast. That is fine **only if** the headline renders without waiting for JS or fonts. TBT/INP still suffer from three.js parse and compile. A static poster `<img>` of the phone (eager, `fetchpriority="high"`) swapped for WebGL after `requestIdleCallback` or first interaction gives an instant visual and protects interactivity. See https://web.dev/articles/lcp.
- How 3D sits relative to the CTA on reference sites:
  - **Apple**: the product is the hero at full width, and the CTA sits in a sticky sub-nav.
  - **Stripe**: text and CTA on the left, mockups on the right; heavy 3D lives lower in the page.
  - **Linear**: headline and CTA first, then a large product image under them.

  In all three, the headline and CTA occupy the first viewport above or beside the 3D and never below it. For the Milan studio, the WebGL iPhone should stay about 40–50% of the hero width on desktop. On mobile it should sit **after** the headline and CTA, or be shrunk enough that the CTA stays above the fold.
- For a 3D phone, the screen content is the persuasive part, not the rotation. Showing a real client "after" site on the phone screen turns the 3D into product demo rather than decoration.
- Interaction should be optional and bounded: a subtle idle float or tilt-on-pointer, no scroll hijacking in the hero, and no drag-required affordances on mobile, where a drag competes with scrolling.

### Gaps
- No primary source confirmed Raycast's or Rive's current hero construction (asset type, size, lazy loading).
- No public numbers on Apple's frame counts or byte budgets per page; the 60–180 figure is from a third-party analysis.
- web.dev LCP documentation could not be fetched this session (DNS failure), so the canvas/LCP rule above comes from prior knowledge.

## Q3. Evidence that interactive demos or 3D raise engagement or conversion, and the pitfalls

### Takeaway
There is no published, independent A/B test isolating a 3D hero's effect on conversion. The available evidence comes from interactive-demo vendors, which measure engagement rather than revenue and are self-interested. Mild motion shows small lifts in one vendor study. The best-supported risk is load time and attention pulled from the CTA, not 3D itself.

### Cited Findings
- **Navattic, State of the Interactive Product Demo 2025** (vendor, 28,000+ demos on its platform): top-1% demos reach a 54.0% CTR, 61.6% completion, 84.4% engagement and 2.1 minutes average time. The top 10% reach a 28.4% CTR and 43.5% completion. These are engagement benchmarks, not revenue. — [Navattic report](https://navattic.com/report/state-of-the-interactive-product-demo-2025); [Navattic benchmarks](https://www.navattic.com/blog/interactive-demos-industry-benchmarks)
- Navattic's 2024 report estimated a **16% website conversion lift** from interactive demos. HockeyStack's own dataset analysis said the lift was larger. — [HockeyStack](https://hockeystack.com/lab-blog-posts/do-interactive-demos-work)
- Walnut (vendor) claims interactive demos convert about 32% higher than static or live-only demos and about 38% higher than screen-share. The full report could not be located. — [Walnut](https://www.walnut.io/blog/product-demos/interactive-demos-conversion-rates-b2b-2026-data/)
- Arcade publishes "top 1%" demo benchmarks for 2025. — [Arcade](https://arcade.software/post/interactive-demo-benchmarks)
- The Unbounce 2025 benchmark, cited secondhand by Guideflow, puts median B2B SaaS landing-page conversion at 3.8%, with the top 10% at 8–15%. — [Guideflow](https://www.guideflow.com/blog/interactive-demo-benchmarks)
- Digital Applied's 2,000-page study (vendor blog, methodology only partly visible): an **animated illustration hero (Lottie/SVG) gave +5%** over a static image-hero control, and a **single-stat hero gave +18%**, the best result. — [Digital Applied](https://www.digitalapplied.com/blog/landing-page-conversion-study-2000-pages-tested-2026)
- CXL research, cited by PageDuel: moving hero elements such as autoplay video backgrounds and animated sliders can distract from the CTA. PageDuel also cites about **7% conversion loss per extra second** of load (secondhand). — [PageDuel](https://pageduel.com/blog/video-landing-page-ab-testing)
- Practitioner advice: always A/B test 3D against a traditional hero, test loop vs on-hover animation as separate variants, and match page weight between variants so the test measures the animation, not speed. — [Lapa Ninja 3D](https://www.lapa.ninja/category/3d-websites/); [Creattie](https://creattie.com/blog/improve-conversions-by-ab-testing)
- Before/after sliders: they help prospects grasp a transformation (remodel, smile, website cleanup), and "one strong comparison does more than six weak ones". Both images need identical dimensions, alignment and crop, and should be optimized. A before/after without behavioral evidence or post-launch metrics "is only a showcase". No measured conversion lift was found. — [Divimode](https://divimode.com/?p=1490349); [Framer Websites guide](https://framerwebsites.com/blog/framer-image-comparison-slider-guide); [Phenomenon Studio](https://phenomenonstudio.com/article-faq/where-can-i-find-portfolio-examples-of-website-redesign-agencies/)

### Inferences
- The evidence that a hero conveying one concrete number or outcome converts better (single-stat hero +18%) is stronger than the evidence for any animation (+5%). The 3D iPhone should carry or sit next to a concrete promise, for example "Nuovo sito in 14 giorni, prezzo fisso", or show a metric on its screen, not stand alone.
- The before/after makeover is the studio's strongest proof device. It gains credibility with one hard number per case, such as load time before and after or calls/bookings, plus the client's name and location. Without numbers it reads as showcase, per Phenomenon Studio.
- The main pitfalls for this site:
  1. WebGL plus the makeover animation competing in the same viewport. Give each its own screen.
  2. Long JS parse on mid-range Android phones that Italian shop owners use, which hurts INP.
  3. Drag or scroll-jacking interactions hiding the CTA.
  4. Autoplay motion that ignores `prefers-reduced-motion`.

### Gaps
- No independent (non-vendor) controlled study measures 3D or WebGL hero impact on conversion. Vendor numbers are self-interested and mostly B2B SaaS, which differs from a local-service lead-gen audience.
- No data on before/after sliders versus static side-by-side images in conversion terms.

## Q4. Italian web agencies and studios targeting PMI: homepage and offer structure

### Takeaway
Italian studios serving PMI and local businesses, Milan included, mostly sell "realizzazione siti" and "restyling" through a quote request, with service pages split by keyword (one page per city or service) for local SEO. Few publish prices or package names. Productized fixed-price, subscription-style framing is uncommon, and where it exists it comes from large platforms (Seat/Pagine Gialle-style, localsearch.ch) rather than solo studios.

### Cited Findings
- Milan freelancers (Andrea Lupi, Griffini, TrattoAsTratto, Graphik Design, MF Web Designer) present restyling for local businesses with portfolios and case studies, mostly without prices, sending visitors to a quote request. — [Andrea Web Designer](https://www.andreawebdesigner.com/); [Griffini](https://griffinidesign.it/); [TrattoAsTratto](https://www.trattoastratto.it/portfolio-web-designer-freelance-milano/); [Graphik Design](https://www.graphikdesign.it/realizzazione-siti-web-pmi-professionisti-milano.html); [MF Web Designer](https://www.mfwebdesignermilano.it/)
- DSI Design is the transparent outlier: vetrina from €1,500+IVA (up to 5 pages), aziendale from €3,000+IVA, and restyling keeps domain and SEO age. — [DSI Design](https://www.dsidesign.it/realizzazione-siti-web-milano/)
- Keyword-per-city landing pages are common. Examples: "Restyling Siti Web Milano" ([andreawebdesigner.com/restyling-siti-web-milano](https://www.andreawebdesigner.com/restyling-siti-web-milano/)) and "Sviluppo siti internet Milano" ([pasqualericciardi.com](https://www.pasqualericciardi.com/siti-lombardia/sviluppo-siti-internet-milano/)).
- A PMI redesign checklist recommends analysing the existing site (Analytics) before design and finishing with 301 redirects and technical tests after launch. — [Seahawk IT](https://seahawkmedia.com/it/progetto/lista-di-controllo-per-la-riprogettazione-del-sito-web-delle-pmi/)
- Historic Italian subscription offers (1&1 MyWebsite free for a year then €10/mo, Telecom "Il mio sito") are dated. A small "€7.9/mo" builder offer (Axiatel) was found with no date. — [01net](https://www.01net.it/?p=148036); [01net Telecom](https://www.01net.it/?p=144514)

### Inferences
- Synthesized homepage order for the Milan one-person studio. This is an inference from the patterns above, not a tested template:
  1. **Hero**: outcome headline in Italian plus a fixed price or "da €X" plus a risk reverser (e.g. "paghi solo se ti piace la bozza"). Primary CTA "Richiedi l'analisi gratuita" and a secondary WhatsApp or call with the founder's face. The WebGL iPhone shows a real client's new site, with a static poster first.
  2. **Proof strip**: client names or shop types in Milan neighbourhoods, plus 1–3 hard numbers.
  3. **Before/after makeover**: one strong case with a metric, then a CTA directly beneath.
  4. **"Come funziona"** in 3 steps with timeline.
  5. **What's included / deliverables**.
  6. **Pricing**: 1–3 fixed packages with a visible price. This differentiates against the quote-only norm.
  7. **More work**, using the live demo viewer, as in Graphik Design's "demo homepages".
  8. **Testimonials** with name, shop and location.
  9. **FAQ** covering costs, hosting, who writes the copy, SEO and domain kept (as DSI stresses).
  10. **Final CTA band**.

  Add a sticky mobile CTA bar (the Apple "Buy" pattern) so motion sections never strand the user without an action.
- Local-SEO practice in Italy suggests also keeping city or neighbourhood service pages (for example "restyling sito ristorante Milano") separate from the conversion-focused homepage.

### Gaps
- No Italian studio homepage could be fetched to confirm exact section order. Structures above come from search excerpts only.
- No Italian-specific conversion data (for example price-visible vs quote-only for PMI) was found.
- Awwwards or Godly-level Italian studios that also sell to PMI were not identified in this session.
