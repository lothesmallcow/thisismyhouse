# Derive every site from its client's world

Focus Design's sites look alike because the starter ships the current version of the "AI look", and a coding assistant left to itself returns to that look. The "same italic" has a name. It is the 2025–2026 generation of AI-site tells: a cream background, a thin serif display face, **one italic or coloured accent word in the headline**, tracked all-caps eyebrow labels, `01/02/03` numbering and `→` on every link. Anthropic's own current `frontend-design` skill now lists these as signs of a generated page ([anthropics/skills](https://github.com/anthropics/skills/blob/main/skills/frontend-design/SKILL.md)). The studio's starter has every core ingredient. `src/styles/tokens.css` sets Newsreader as the display face on `#f2f1ec` (almost exactly the `#F4F1EA` cream Anthropic flags), and `src/components/Hero.astro` always prints the second line of the hero title as `<em>` set in italic. The design-brief skill's avoid-list covers only the older purple-gradient/Inter generation, and it offers three fixed hero components. Banning fonts does not fix this, because models just move to the next most likely default. The fix is a written derivation step per client: three objects from the business's world, the place, a palette sampled from the client's photos, one layout archetype, one signature element and two or three hard constraints, all checked against the studio's last three sites. Dead space has a different cause. It comes from layout mechanics: stacked padding, fixed column counts meeting odd content counts, short text next to tall images, and desktop padding copied to mobile. Because those mechanisms are geometric, a Playwright script can measure them at 390 and 1440 px. Evidence quality is uneven. The tell catalogues are practitioner consensus backed by two small samples (120 pages, 72 sites), the spacing numbers are convention rather than research, and none of the sector reference sites could be opened live during the research.

**Evidence marks.** **[E]** means a primary document, standard or direct inspection of the studio's files. **[P]** means practitioner, vendor or small informal samples. **[S]** means synthesis and recommendation for this studio, not sourced.

## The "same italic" is 2026's AI tell, and the starter ships it

There are two generations of AI look. **Generation 1 (2024 to mid-2025)** is the one everyone mocks: Inter, a purple-to-blue gradient on white, a centred hero with a pill badge and two buttons, three icon cards and glassmorphism. Anthropic described the unguided result as "Inter fonts, purple gradients on white backgrounds, and minimal animations" ([Anthropic](https://claude.com/blog/improving-frontend-design-through-skills)). OpenAI's guidance tells its models to avoid "purple-on-white defaults" and, notably, **beige/cream/sand/tan** palettes, and to "scan the CSS colors before finalizing" ([OpenAI](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/guides/frontend-prompt)). **Generation 2 (late 2025 to 2026)** grew out of the anti-slop advice itself. It is the "tasteful default", which Impeccable describes as "a cream page, thin serif headline, italic accent, and pill buttons" ([Impeccable](https://github.com/pbakaus/impeccable)). Anthropic's current skill names its pieces explicitly. It flags "accenting just a single word or phrase in a headline, like putting one word in italic/bold or a different color", "using all caps for labels", and "a warm cream background (near #F4F1EA) with a high-contrast serif display and a terracotta or warm-clay accent (often near #D97757)". It also flags template chrome: a tracked-out eyebrow above every heading, `A · B · C` meta strings, mono micro-labels and "a '→' appended to link and button text" ([anthropics/skills](https://github.com/anthropics/skills/blob/main/skills/frontend-design/SKILL.md)). Impeccable added a detector rule, `italic-serif-display`, that fires on any h1 set in an italic serif of 48 px or more. Its list of serifs includes Fraunces, Playfair, Cormorant and Newsreader, and the pull request calls the pattern "category-defining" for late 2025 to early 2026 ([Impeccable PR #129](https://github.com/pbakaus/impeccable/pull/129)). **[E]**

The studio's own files show where the sameness comes from. In `studio-starter`, the display font is `'Newsreader Variable'` and the background is `#f2f1ec`. The hero title is rendered as `{title[0]} <em>{title[1]}</em>`, with `.hero__title em { display: block; font-style: italic; }`, so every client inherits an italic second line unless someone removes it. The hero also defaults to full screen (`--hero-peek: 0rem`). The design-brief skill's avoid-list stops at Generation 1 (purple gradients, Inter, Space Grotesk, cream + terracotta, eyebrows, 01/02/03) and tells the model to "pick HeroA, HeroB or HeroC". That gives each site one of three heroes. The studio's sales site, `focale`, has Fraunces and Cormorant Garamond among its font dependencies, and both are on 2026 tell lists. In short, the template supplies the convergence and the assistant reinforces it. **[E]** (direct inspection of `src/styles/tokens.css`, `src/components/Hero.astro`, `.claude/skills/design-brief/SKILL.md`, `focale/package.json`)

The mechanism explains why swapping fonts will not be enough. Anthropic calls it distributional convergence: "Safe design choices… dominate web training data," and "without direction, Claude samples from this high-probability center" ([Anthropic](https://claude.com/blog/improving-frontend-design-through-skills)). That same November 2025 post recommended Playfair Display, Newsreader, Space Grotesk and Bricolage Grotesque. By mid-2026 all four were on tell lists. This is the "next attractor" problem: ban a default and the model "picks the very next overused font in its training weights" ([BigGo on Bakaus](https://finance.biggo.com/news/71cfafb357af64ab)). Instrument Serif became common precisely because it was the standard recommended "fix" ([The Crit](https://thecrit.co/resources/vibe-coding-design-guide)). Component kits make it worse, since v0 is reportedly trained on default shadcn components ([DEV: 72 sites graded](https://dev.to/landingpagegods/why-do-ai-generated-websites-all-look-the-same-we-graded-72-2026-300c)). The quantitative evidence is thin. Build to Launch counted 16 habits across **120 AI-built pages**: icon-on-top feature cards on **63%** of landing pages, the Lucide icon set on 63%, default shadcn colours on 61%, and **57 of 120 pages showing six or more signs** ([Build to Launch](https://buildtolaunch.substack.com/p/fix-ai-slop-website-design)). The author's conclusion is the useful part: no single sign gives a page away, the combination does. No large corpus study or peer-reviewed work on visual mode collapse exists. **[P]**

For a studio with one coding assistant, the conclusion is uncomfortable. **Any fixed list of "good fonts" will itself converge** once the same assistant uses it for every client. Even the earlier recommendation of Schibsted Grotesk for the studio's own site is right for that site, but it would become a house tell if it spread to client sites. What the studio needs is a process that produces different inputs for each client. **[S]**

Copy has its own tells, and the most damaging ones for Italian small businesses are those that invent facts. The lists agree on "Not X, but Y" constructions, reflexive triplets, and verbs such as elevate, seamless and unlock ([FeedHive](https://github.com/FeedHive/ai-slop-detector/blob/main/skills/ai-slop-detector/SKILL.md); [Here Forward](https://www.hereforward.ai/learn/ai-tells)). A fair counter-view holds that em dashes and the rule of three are legitimate. What gives copy away is "the pile": dashes, a triplet, "elevate" and no specifics together ([J.R. Leach](https://jrleach.substack.com/p/in-defence-of-the-em-dash)). Invented proof such as "10,000+ satisfied customers" and placeholder testimonials appears on every list ([Noqode](https://www.noqode.fr/en/blog/design-ia-generique)), and the studio's CLAUDE.md already forbids it. The Italian equivalents below are my translation, since no source covers Italian AI copy specifically. **[P]/[S]**

| Tell | Why it reads as generated | Replace with |

Measured against the current tell lists, the eyebrow is acceptable once, because it carries information (sectors and Milan). The arrow should be dropped. Eyebrows above every later section heading should go. **[S]**

## Personality comes from a derivation step, not a better font list

Every practitioner source agrees on one principle: **start from the subject, not from a style.** Anthropic's skill says visual choices should come from the subject's "industry, subject matter, materials, and vernacular", and that the hero should open with "the most characteristic thing in the subject's world". Its process is to write a compact token system first (4–6 named hex colours, typefaces with roles, a layout concept as an ASCII wireframe), then review the plan against the brief and revise anything generic, then build, then critique with screenshots using Chanel's rule of removing one accessory, because "a single orchestrated moment… lands better than scattered effects" ([anthropics/skills](https://github.com/anthropics/skills/blob/main/skills/frontend-design/SKILL.md)). The `avoid-ai-design` project adds a test for every choice: "would I have done this for any similar page?" It also asks that colours come "from the product's materials and artifacts" ([avoid-ai-design](https://github.com/funboy322/avoid-ai-design)). Paul Bakaus's anti-attractor technique deals with the next-attractor problem directly. The model must "enumerate and reject its reflex defaults before picking", for example by naming its top three fonts and discarding them, and generation is seeded with something the model would not choose, such as a curated palette or a random reference ([Impeccable](https://github.com/pbakaus/impeccable); [Bakaus on X](https://x.com/pbakaus/status/2044505743144194514?lang=en)). Bakaus reports "much more font and color diversity" from this, but only in his own self-reported eval. He also admits his judging sub-agents do "only slightly better than chance" at taste. Human review stays in the loop. **[E]/[P]**

In practice, the studio's design brief should become a derivation sheet that the model fills in before it writes any CSS. **[S]** It should list three physical things from the business's world, such as a reformer's steel springs and leather straps, an agriturismo's cotto floor and painted ceramic house number, a wedding mass booklet, or a renovation firm's *cartello di cantiere* and technical drawings. It should name the place and period (Milan Liberty, a 1950s *palazzina*, a Lombard *cascina*) and the local lettering: enamel street signs, shop fascias, inscriptions. Colours should be sampled from the client's own photos (three bases and one accent) and named after their source in `tokens.css`, for example `--color-ferro`, `--color-cotto` or `--color-salvia-giardino`, so the token file documents where they came from. The sheet should name one signature element taken from that world, hold a rejection list of the model's first-choice font, palette and layout with the reason each was dropped, and set two or three hard constraints. Constraints push the model off its centre more reliably than adjectives like "elegante" or "moderno". Examples are "no rounded corners, because the brand is ironwork", "two colours only, as on the client's van", "no cards", and "no eyebrows; section names go in the heading". The last field is a diff against the studio's previous three sites: no shared display face, palette family, layout archetype or hero component. Place-based identity guidance supports tying the visual system to local landscape, geology and building styles ([Silphium Design](https://silphiumdesign.com/brand-consistency-place-based-visual-identity/)). Using Milanese vernacular lettering in particular is my extrapolation, since no source studied it.

Layout archetypes should be assigned deliberately rather than inherited, replacing the default hero → three cards → stats → testimonials → CTA sequence. The table maps them to the studio's sectors. It is a recommendation, not a sourced taxonomy. **[S]**

| Archetype | What it is | Natural fit | Signature element example |
|---|---|---|---|
| Index / timetable | The weekly class grid or list *is* the hero, set as real typography | Pilates, fitness | Timetable in a condensed face; a spring/coil line drawing as divider |
| Full-bleed photo narrative | Rooms, breakfast and landscape in sequence, sparse text | B&B, agriturismo | A tile-pattern band or a hand-drawn farm map |
| Editorial single column | Long-form story of the family or the house, generous measure | Agriturismo, family venue | A pull-quote from the owners (real, from the client) |
| Document / artefact | The page laid out like the business's own object: an invitation, a *menu del giorno*, a *preventivo* | Wedding venue, renovation | Invitation-card hero with letterpress spacing |
| Plan / heritage | A plan of the grounds with named spaces and per-space capacity in a typographic table | Wedding venue, villa | Site plan drawn as a single-line map |
| Catalogue / technical sheet | Projects as an indexed archive with plain captions (place, material, year) | Renovation, joinery, window fitters | A *cartello di cantiere* info panel (project, address, dates) |
| Poster | One huge typographic statement and one photo | Renovation, event venue | Display type cut to the site's real address or street |
| Split screen | Photo fixed on one side, content scrolling on the other | Venues with strong architecture | Sticky photo that changes per section |

Type should be chosen for a role tied to the derivation, never as a default. The sources do not name Italian-suited families, so the directions below are my recommendations. All are open-licensed and available through Fontsource, which matters because the studio forbids Google Fonts requests and Anthropic's November 2025 post still says "Load from Google Fonts" ([Anthropic](https://claude.com/blog/improving-frontend-design-through-skills)). Fontshare faces such as Satoshi use a non-OFL licence and should not be redistributed ([licenseorg](https://licenseorg.com/guide/fonts/fontshare)). There are two documented Italian-heritage options. **Titillium Web** came out of the Accademia di Belle Arti di Urbino ecosystem and is used in Italian public-administration design ([Designers Italia](https://medium.com/designers-italia/un-carattere-per-l-identit%C3%A0-italiana-c704f3e4791d)), which suits a renovation firm's technical register. **Bodoni Moda** is an OFL revival of Giambattista Bodoni's Parma Didone ([FontFYI](https://fontfyi.com/font/bodoni-moda/)), which suits a wedding venue when it is set upright with real hierarchy and not as one italic word. Beyond those, the families I would rotate are these. Grotesques with character: Familjen, Schibsted, Hanken, Host, Funnel, Archivo (its width axis gives condensed headings and normal text from one family). Condensed signage faces for timetables and prices: Big Shoulders Display, Barlow Condensed, Sofia Sans Extra Condensed, Antonio. Slab serifs for workshop and rural brands: Zilla Slab, Bitter, Aleo, Rokkitt. Less common text serifs: Literata, Source Serif 4, Spectral, Alegreya, Cardo, EB Garamond with real small caps. Monospace only where data is tabular: IBM Plex Mono for price lists and specs. Inscriptional display for one element only: Tenor Sans or Marcellus rather than Cinzel, which is the wedding cliché. The pairing strategies are a superfamily with width or weight contrast, slab with grotesque, condensed display with humanist text, an upright Didone with a plain grotesque, or one family carrying all hierarchy through size and weight. A useful precedent from hotel identities on Fonts In Use is **a contemporary serif with character paired with a neutral grotesque** (Reforma 1918 with Canela at Desértica; Right Serif with TWK Lausanne at Casa Llimona), rather than the default "Playfair + Montserrat" ([Fonts In Use: Desértica](https://fontsinuse.com/uses/24874/desertica-hotel); [Fonts In Use: Casa Llimona](https://fontsinuse.com/uses/60627/casa-llimona-hotel)). **[E]** for licence and heritage facts, **[S]** for the lists.

Colour follows the same rule. "Dominant colors with sharp accents outperform timid, evenly-distributed palettes" ([Anthropic](https://claude.com/blog/improving-frontend-design-through-skills)), so the pattern is one dominant colour sampled from the place (stone, plaster, cotto, ironwork, lake light) and one sharp accent. The named tell families should stay out unless the brief really demands them: cream with clay, sage or teal on cream, near-black with acid green, and indigo or purple ([anthropics/skills](https://github.com/anthropics/skills/blob/main/skills/frontend-design/SKILL.md); [Unslop UI](https://unslopui.org/blog/best-fonts-colors-avoid-ai-look-saas-landing-page)). Anthropic's own caveat applies to everything in this section. These patterns are legitimate for some briefs and become a problem only when they appear by default whatever the subject. An editorial wedding venue may want a serif display. It should get one because of the villa, not because of the starter. **[E]/[S]**

## Dead space is a layout mechanism, so it can be measured

No authoritative source defines "dead space". In practitioner usage it is empty area that looks accidental: it does not group, separate, frame or point to anything, and it often sits next to a crowded area ([Vox Illustration](https://voxillustration.com/blog/dead-space-in-graphic-design-difference-between-dead-space-and-whitespace/); [jezweb design-review](https://github.com/jezweb/claude-skills/blob/main/plugins/frontend/skills/design-review/SKILL.md)). That gives a usable review question: for each empty area, does it group, separate, frame or point? If none applies, it is dead space. Refactoring UI makes the complementary point that "you don't have to fill the whole screen". A 600 px paragraph under a 1,200 px nav is fine, and it is better to start with too much space and remove it than to add space until nothing looks "actively bad" ([Refactoring UI excerpt](https://studylib.net/doc/27063975/pdfcoffee.com-refactoring-ui-v102pdf-3-pdf-free)). **[P]**

On LLM-built sites, dead space comes from seven recurring mechanisms **[S]**, built from the cited cases: **orphan cards** (a 1-of-3 last row, or `repeat(auto-fill, …)` tracks that stay empty, since `auto-fit` collapses empty tracks and `auto-fill` keeps them ([MDN](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/CSS/Reference/Values/repeat))); **height mismatch in splits**, where short text beside a tall image leaves a blank block under the text; **padding-dominated sections**, such as 2×120 px around a two-line paragraph; **double padding**, where section, wrapper and component each add their own, so adjacent sections look fine alone and empty together ([Elegant Themes](https://www.elegantthemes.com/blog/divi-resources/mobile-responsive-design-with-divi-5); [FrontFixer](https://frontfixer.com/section-leaving-big-blank-space/)); **the empty right half**, a narrow text block left-aligned in a 1,440 px container with nothing beside it; **forced `100vh`/`100svh` sections** with little content; and **desktop padding copied to the 390 px viewport**. Each mechanism has a structural fix. Knowing them in advance matters more than any single pixel value.

### Spacing tokens: few values, strict proximity, padding at one level

The starter's 8 px-grid tokens (`--space-4` to `--space-128`) are already in line with practice: about 6–10 values on a 4/8 base, with no off-scale numbers ([Pixso](https://pixso.net/articles/8-point-grid-spacing-system/); [refactoring-ui-skill](https://github.com/s0xDk/refactoring-ui-skill/blob/main/SKILL.md)). What is missing is relationships. Proximity is the one principle here with research behind it: "items close together are likely to be perceived as part of the same group", so space between groups must clearly exceed space within them, and a heading must sit closer to its own content than to the previous section ([NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/gestalt-proximity/); [NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/visual-hierarchy-ux-definition/)). I translate that into a testable ratio: **gap between groups ≥ 2× gap within the group**, following the 16 → 32 → 64 progression, and gap above a heading > gap below it. **[E]** for the principle, **[S]** for the ratio.

Section padding should be a single fluid token. Practitioner examples cluster between a mobile floor of 48–64 px and a desktop ceiling of 96–120 px, for example `clamp(48px, 8vw, 96px)` or a 64 → 120 px ramp ([FrontFixer](https://frontfixer.com/section-leaving-big-blank-space/); [Elegant Themes](https://www.elegantthemes.com/blog/divi-resources/how-to-build-a-fluid-spacing-ramp-with-clamp-in-divi-5)). I recommend `--section-pad: clamp(3rem, 2rem + 4vw, 6rem)` (48 → 96 px) as the default, a larger variant up to 128 px reserved for one or two deliberate pauses per page, and 64–80 px maximum on list or blog pages. Padding should live on `section` only, never also on `.container`. Two adjacent sections with the same background should share padding rather than double it. Utopia's fluid "space pairs" (`--space-xl-2xl`) are a ready-made way to generate this ([Utopia](https://utopia.fyi/space/calculator/)). No controlled study gives an optimal section padding. Every number here is convention. **[P]/[S]**

Vertical rhythm is now cheap. `lh` and `rlh` units have worked in all browsers since 2023, so a single `.flow > * + * { margin-block-start: 1rlh }` rule aligns headings, paragraphs and captions. Headings get more space above (2rlh) than below (0.5rlh), and CTAs get 1.5rlh before them ([Vincent Bernat](https://vincent.bernat.ch/en/blog/2026-css-vertical-rhythm); [Paweł Grzybek](https://pawelgrzybek.com/vertical-rhythm-using-css-lh-and-rlh-units/)). Body line-height should stay unitless and at least 1.5 ([MDN](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/CSS/Reference/Properties/line-height)), and the measure should be about 65ch ([Baymard](https://baymard.com/blog/line-length-readability)). The starter's 1.6 and 62ch already comply. A strict baseline grid is optional polish and does not prevent dead space. **[E]**

### Let content counts choose the grid

Orphans happen when a fixed column count meets real content counts. The CSS fixes are well documented. `auto-fit` with `minmax()` lets leftovers stretch. `:nth-child()` combined with `:last-child` detects a widow and offsets it to look centred ([CSS { In Real Life }](https://css-irl.info/controlling-leftover-grid-items/)). Doubling the track count and spanning 2 centres a single orphan ([Bill Erickson](https://www.billerickson.net/css-grid-center-last-item/)). A native "last item in a row" selector is still only a CSSWG proposal ([W3C, March 2026](https://lists.w3.org/Archives/Public/public-css-archive/2026Mar/0917.html)). **[E]** The studio's architecture offers something simpler. Because the facts live in `services.json`, `team.json` and `gallery.json`, the Astro component knows `items.length` at build time and can choose the layout: multiples of 3 go in 3 columns, multiples of 4 in 4 or 2×2, 5 becomes 3+2 with the pair centred (or one large card and four), and other counts use a centred flex-wrap. That is more robust than any CSS trick and costs nothing at runtime. **[S]**

Two-column splits need a rule as well. Grid's default `align-items: stretch` equalises column heights, so an image set to `width:100%; height:100%; object-fit:cover` matches the text ([CodeFronts](https://codefronts.com/layouts/css-two-column-layout/css-two-column-layout-equal-height/)). The alternatives are a fixed `aspect-ratio` with `align-items: center`, or, for long text, a sticky image with `align-self: start`, which sticky elements need in order to move ([WebmasterWorld](https://www.webmasterworld.com/css/5099620.htm)). As a rule of thumb, the column height ratio should stay between **0.67 and 1.5**. Outside that range, change the aspect ratio, add real content, or stack the columns. **[P]/[S]**

### At 390 px, padding and stacked cards are the enemy

Material 3's compact class uses 16 dp margins ([1440px.com](https://1440px.com/breakpoints/material-design/)), and practitioner mobile floors for section padding are 32–64 px ([clampgenerator](https://clampgenerator.com/guides/layout-spacing-padding-margin-gap-css-clamp/)). For 390 px that means inline margins of 16–24 px, section padding of 48–64 px, and anything above 80 px treated as a red flag unless it is a deliberate pause. Sets of 3–6 peer cards (services, team, reviews) become a scroll-snap rail at about 80–85% card width, so the next card visibly peeks ([envydesign](https://envydesign.jp/patterns/en/carousel-peek/)). Price lists and opening hours stay stacked, because people compare them ([Suleiman](https://blog.iamsuleiman.com/horizontal-scrolling-lists-mobile-best-practices/)). In a desktop split, the image goes above the text at a shorter aspect ratio (4:3 rather than 4:5), so the phone does not show a full screen of image. `100vh` misreports on mobile, so heroes should use `min-height: 100svh`, and only when headline, CTA and image actually fill it ([Web Designer Wall](https://webdesignerwall.com/tutorials/css-fix-for-ios-vh-unit-bug)). The starter's full-screen default (`--hero-peek: 0rem`) conflicts with the earlier finding that a first screen which looks complete makes visitors assume nothing is below. That finding argued for capping heroes at about 85–90% of the viewport, so the next section peeks above the fold ([NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/videos/illusion-completeness/)). Make the peek the default. Allow full screen only when the client brief states a reason. **[E]/[P]/[S]**

### Ten checks a Playwright script can run on every build

No off-the-shelf dead-space detector exists, but the needed APIs do. Playwright's `boundingBox()` behaves like `getBoundingClientRect()`. Empty or `display:none` elements have no box and must be filtered out. The deprecated layout pseudo-classes (`:right-of`, `:near`) should not be used ([Playwright](https://playwright.dev/docs/api/class-locator); [Playwright](https://playwright.dev/docs/other-locators)). An academic paper on LLM design evaluation lists inter-element distance and spacing consistency as measurable signals of white-space quality ([arXiv 2410.08885](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2410.08885)). The thresholds below are **untested starting points**. Tune them on the studio's existing sites before failing builds on them. **[E]** for the APIs, **[S]** for the checks.

| # | Check (viewports 390×844 and 1440×900) | Fails when |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Padding share: (section height − union of visible content boxes) / section height | > 0.5 at 1440, > 0.4 at 390 |
| 2 | Largest full-width vertical gap with no content (excluding background images) | > 160 px at 1440, > 96 px at 390 |
| 3 | Horizontal emptiness: content x-extent vs container width | < 55% coverage and off-centre by > 20% of the container |
| 4 | Grid orphans: group children by `top` into rows and compare the last row with the track count from `gridTemplateColumns` | Short last row left-aligned, or empty auto-fill tracks |
| 5 | Split mismatch in 2-child rows | Height ratio outside 0.67–1.5, or > 120 px blank under the shorter column |
| 6 | Viewport-height sections | `min-height` ≥ 90% of viewport and content < 50% of section |
| 7 | Ink coverage per scrolled viewport (pixels differing from the section background) | < 8–10% below the hero |
| 8 | Proximity: heading gap below vs gap above; gap inside a card stack vs gap between cards | Below ≥ above; inside ≥ between |
| 9 | Off-scale spacing: computed margin, padding and gap vs the `tokens.css` set (±1 px) | Any value off the scale (enforces the CLAUDE.md token rule) |
| 10 | Squint artefact: blurred screenshot (`filter: blur(8px)` on `html`) saved next to the normal one | Human or LLM review: blobs do not match the intended hierarchy, crowded area next to empty one ([jezweb](https://github.com/jezweb/claude-skills/blob/main/plugins/frontend/skills/design-review/SKILL.md)) |

Once a page passes review, its screenshots should become `toHaveScreenshot` baselines, so later edits that reintroduce gaps show up as diffs ([Playwright #14233](https://github.com/microsoft/playwright/issues/14233)). Running the AI-tell detectors on the built `dist/` closes the other loop. `npx impeccable detect` runs in CI and includes `italic-serif-display`, `hero-eyebrow-chip` and cramped-padding rules ([Impeccable](https://github.com/pbakaus/impeccable)), and slop-detect measures the *ratio* of headings with eyebrows rather than flagging every one ([slop-detect #229](https://github.com/ravidsrk/slop-detect/issues/229)). Neither tool has been tested against Italian-language Astro output yet. **[P]**

## Sector references: borrow mechanisms, not looks

One caveat governs this whole section. During the research, **every reference page failed to load**. Award dates, agencies and typeface names come from search snippets of Awwwards, Fonts In Use and agency pages. Palettes, layouts and signature elements were not seen, and some sites may have been redesigned. Open each site and screenshot it before using it as a reference. What transfers safely is the mechanism each one uses, not its surface.

**Pilates and fitness.** **Core Atelier Pilates** won an Awwwards Honorable Mention on 25 March 2026 (Greydient Lab). It is credited with scroll-driven typography, a "personalised journey" quiz and a **slide-in contact panel** reachable from anywhere on the page ([Awwwards](https://www.awwwards.com/sites/core-atelier-pilates); [Awwwards: slide-in panel](https://www.awwwards.com/inspiration/slide-in-contact-panel-core-atelier-pilates)). CASA CORPO and YOGAMAYA received Honorable Mentions in 2025 ([Awwwards yoga](https://www.awwwards.com/inspiration_search/yoga/); [Awwwards: YOGAMAYA](https://www.awwwards.com/sites/yogamaya)). The identity projects are more instructive. **The Kay Pilates Studio** sets its logo in Relais with modified A and Y letterforms about movement and alignment ([Fonts In Use](https://fontsinuse.com/uses/55047/the-kay-pilates-studio)). **Corporis** draws its geometric logo from the shapes of the Polestar Pilates machine ([Fonts In Use](https://fontsinuse.com/uses/40727/corporis-pilates-studio)). Strength studios such as Gymtown (Power Grotesk with Instrument Sans, carried from website to gym signage) and Hench (Alte Haas Grotesk) use heavy grotesques ([Fonts In Use: Gymtown](https://fontsinuse.com/uses/62242/gymtown); [Fonts In Use: Hench](https://fontsinuse.com/uses/22390/hench-identity-and-website)). None of them uses beige with a thin serif. A Milan studio can take three things from these. First, a persistent booking panel that opens WhatsApp or `tel:` with no third-party widget. Second, a static "quale lezione fa per me" quiz in Astro. Third, a motif drawn from the studio's own equipment or room instead of lotus icons. The timetable-as-hero archetype fits a pilates studio better than any photo. **[P]/[S]**

**B&B and agriturismo.** The strongest Italian references come from Positioner. **Le Sirenuse** (Awwwards Honorable Mention and Mobile Excellence, April 2021) rests, in the agency's words, on "highly immersive visuals, taken by our recommended photographer" rather than a complex user experience ([Positioner](https://www.positioner.com/hotel-brand-design/le-sirenuse)). **Passalacqua** on Lake Como (Honorable Mention) publishes "only the start of the stories" on the site and runs a "Voices" editorial section ([Positioner: Passalacqua](https://www.positioner.com/hotel-brand-design/passalacqua); [Passalacqua Voices](https://www.passalacqua.it/en/voices/it-s-not-just-about-design-it-s-about-curating-an-experience/)). Grand Hotel Miramare received an Honorable Mention in March 2024 ([Awwwards](https://www.awwwards.com/sites/grand-hotel-miramare)). **Masseria Moroseta** in Ostuni is an architecture-led reference, with web design credited to Folch ([Openhouse](https://openhouse-magazine.com/studio/masseria-moroseta-hotel/)). Typography tied to the building is the transferable idea: the Hermitage Hotel's custom face is drawn from its Beaux-Arts interiors, and another hotel's Amalia has a stencil cut for door signs ([Fonts In Use](https://fontsinuse.com/uses/49309/the-hermitage-hotel); [Fontstand](https://fontstand.com/news/fonts-in-use/fonts-in-use-sleeping-over/)). For a B&B budget that means one coherent photo set shot in a single season and light, two or three short real stories (the people, the land, the house), and lettering taken from the farm's old signs or wine labels. No agriturismo with a design award was found. **[P]/[S]**

**Wedding venues.** The research found **no award-recognised venue website from 2022–2026**. Wedding-tagged Awwwards winners are couples' sites ([Awwwards wedding](https://www.awwwards.com/inspiration_search/wedding/)). The agency case studies that do exist describe the cliché: a "timeless serif" name with a handwritten-script location in creamy whites and muted blues ([Honě Creative](https://honecreativestudio.com/2025/10/01/wedding-venue-logo/)), or a romantic script, rounded edges and an embedded availability calendar ([Matt Gerber Designs](https://mattgerberdesigns.com/portfolio-posts/the-bowery-barn/)). Those make useful anti-references. One real user finding is worth more than any gallery: a usability study of a venue site found **visitors wanted pricing and booking online** ([Biltmore Barn case study](https://www.brianaboitano.com/biltmore-barn-website-case-study)), although it was a student project. The distinctive direction has to be built by analogy with the hotel references. Treat the villa as a heritage subject. Show a plan of the grounds with named spaces (cortile, limonaia, sala) and per-space capacity in a typographic table. Tell the house's history as an editorial story. Choose a typeface from the building's period instead of a script. Be transparent about capacity, dates and indicative price, with any missing figure written as `[TODO]`. Replace a third-party calendar embed with a static "verifica disponibilità" form or WhatsApp link, since an embed would break the no-third-party rule. **[P]/[S]**

**Renovation, joinery and window fitters.** Trade sites rarely win top awards. The documented Honorable Mentions include Thomas May Bespoke Joinery (2018) and **NorthShield Windows and Doors**, the closest match to a *serramentista* brief, with its date unconfirmed ([Awwwards: Thomas May](https://www.awwwards.com/sites/thomas-may-bespoke-joinery); [Awwwards: NorthShield](https://www.awwwards.com/sites/northshield-windows-and-doors)). The identity work is stronger. **FABB Carpentry** pairs KMR Apparat Bold with FK Grotesk Neue ([Fonts In Use](https://fontsinuse.com/uses/56510/fabb-carpentry-identity)), and **Hea** is led by a bespoke lumber-inspired typeface ([The Brand Identity](https://the-brandidentity.com/project/studio-bruchs-identity-for-carpentry-company-hea-is-led-by-a-bespoke-lumber-inspired-typeface)). In both, the type itself carries the craft signal. The transferable structure is the catalogue or technical sheet: projects indexed with plain captions ("Lavoro 014 – Finestre in rovere, Monza", but only with real project data), macro photos of joints and profiles, a short "come lavoriamo" process, and a phone/WhatsApp "sopralluogo" CTA in the header. Call it free only if the client confirms that. Hard hats, blueprints, navy with safety orange, "il vostro partner di fiducia" and "500+ progetti" counters are the sector's tells. The last also breaks the studio's rule against invented facts. **[P]/[S]**

All four sectors point the same way. Distinctive small-business sites win with **one coherent photographic direction, typography tied to the place or the trade, and a structure borrowed from the business's own documents** (timetable, story, plan, catalogue). Technical effects matter less. That is good news for a static Astro studio, because none of these mechanisms needs JavaScript or third-party requests.

## Conclusion

The research changes the diagnosis. "All our sites look the same" sounds like a taste problem, but the files show it is a pipeline problem. The starter encodes one client's art direction (Casa Turati's cream, Newsreader and italic line), the brief offers three heroes and an outdated avoid-list, and the assistant's own priors pull every new project back to the same centre. A better template will not fix this, because any fixed choice becomes the next default once it is reused. What works is making the inputs differ: a derivation from the client's objects and place, a rejection of the model's first picks, constraints, and a diff against the last three sites. The starter's neutral state should be visibly plain, so that no client can ship it unchanged.

Dead space is the more tractable half, because it is geometry. Two habits remove most of it before any check runs: put padding on one level only, and let `items.length` from the content JSON choose the grid. The Playwright checks then turn "spacing feels off" into numbers the assistant can fix on its own before Paolo or Lorenzo ever sees a screenshot. The open questions are empirical. The thresholds need tuning on the studio's existing sites, the sector references need a live look, and only Bakaus's self-reported eval suggests the derivation process measurably increases diversity. Logging each delivered site's fonts, palette family and archetype will show within a few projects whether the sites are actually diverging.

## Checklist for every new site

Priority 1 items are blocking: the site does not ship without them. Priority 2 items are changes to the studio's tools that make Priority 1 cheaper. Priority 3 items are follow-ups.

### Priority 1, before any CSS (in `/design-brief`)

| # | Step | Done when |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Derivation sheet: 3 physical objects from the business, place and period, local lettering, 6–10 references from the client's own photos and printed material | Written in the brief, each cue with a reason it belongs |
| 2 | Rejection list: the model writes its first-choice display font, palette and layout, then discards them | Three rejected choices recorded |
| 3 | Palette: 4–6 colours sampled from the client's photos and place, named after their source, one dominant plus one sharp accent; none of cream + clay/sage/teal, near-black + acid accent, indigo/purple unless the brief justifies it | Named tokens with hex and contrast ratios |
| 4 | Type: 1–2 families with stated roles, available on Fontsource under OFL; no Inter, Geist, Space Grotesk, Instrument Serif, Fraunces, Playfair, Cormorant or Newsreader as a default; italics only for their real job | Roles table; licence checked |
| 5 | One layout archetype (timetable, photo narrative, editorial, document, plan, catalogue, poster, split) with an ASCII wireframe per page, mobile first | Archetype named and justified from step 1 |
| 6 | One signature element from the business's world, and 2–3 hard constraints | Written; the "remove one accessory" pass done |
| 7 | Diff against the studio's last 3 sites: no shared display face, palette family, archetype or hero component | Comparison written |
| 8 | Copy: headline says what, where and for whom; no triplet taglines, "Non solo X, ma Y", "esperienza unica", "a 360 gradi", "su misura", "immergiti"; no numbers, stars or reviews not in the content JSON (`[TODO]` instead) | Copy reviewed against the list |
