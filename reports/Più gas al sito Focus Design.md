# Trade twenty details for one focus pull

Focus Design's site lacks "gas" because its effort is spread thin across many small effects, and none of them belongs to the name, not because it has too few. The current home stacks a WebGL iPhone hero, Lenis smooth scrolling, GSAP, a staggered mask reveal on almost every block, a blue label above every section heading and a five-tile icon grid. The one motif built on the name, headings that sharpen from a blur, runs only on inner pages and only with a mouse, so phone visitors never see it. The evidence points the other way. Award-recognised studio sites win with **one signature idea carried all the way through**: the clearest 2025 precedent is a CSS-only portfolio that won Awwwards Site of the Day ([Awwwards](https://www.awwwards.com/sites/cyd-stumpel-portfolio-2025)). Productized studios look premium because the offer itself is confident: one price, one deadline, one call to action. Apple's punch comes mostly from scale, stillness and one idea per screen, with motion kept for a few staged moments. On conversions, the tested evidence favours familiar, low-complexity layouts, which people judge within tens of milliseconds ([Google Research](https://research.google/blog/users-love-simple-and-familiar-designs-why-websites-need-to-make-a-great-first-impression/)), and it is negative on scrolljacking ([NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/scrolljacking-101/)). The recommendation is to subtract first, then spend the saved motion and weight budget on one signature, **"Messa a fuoco"**: the site in the hero phone starts out of focus and snaps sharp once while viewfinder corner marks close in. The same corner marks then recur in three or four places. All of it is opacity and transform in plain CSS. Because the studio stays "noi" with no founder photo, trust has to come from the open price, the deadline, real work and a dry, confident voice in the small print. Two limits apply. No study shows that a signature interaction lifts leads for a service business, and none of the reference sites could be opened live during this research. Everything below is therefore built from search snippets, published guidance and direct inspection of the studio's own files.

**Evidence marks.** **[E]** means a primary document, standard, measured study or direct inspection of the studio's files (`/home/user/thisismyhouse/focale/`). **[P]** means practitioner, vendor, juror or press sources. **[S]** means synthesis and recommendation for this studio, not sourced.

## Award and productized sites both win with one idea done fully

The verified award examples follow one pattern: a single strong move, carried out completely. **Cyd Stumpel's Portfolio 2025 won Site of the Day on 9 March 2025.** Awwwards describes it as "built with the newest CSS techniques: View Transitions and Scroll Driven Animations" ([Awwwards](https://www.awwwards.com/sites/cyd-stumpel-portfolio-2025)). It is the closest precedent for a framework-light Astro site, because it shows that award-level excitement needs neither WebGL nor heavy JavaScript. Rauno Freiberg's 2025 portfolio earned an Honorable Mention, and its tagged standout elements are just two: horizontal scroll and a minimap ([Awwwards](https://www.awwwards.com/inspiration/horizontal-scroll-rauno-portfolio-2025)). Usability counts even at Awwwards. A November 2025 Site of the Day listing shows the jury weighting as **design 40%, usability 30%, creativity 20%, content 10%** ([Awwwards](https://www.awwwards.com/sites/portfolio-25-1)). A juror writes that sites which collapse on phones stall at Honorable Mention, and that points are lost for "scattered effects [that] read as noise", for missing focus states or reduced-motion paths, and for "a custom cursor that hides the real one" ([Hon Tran](https://www.hontran.dev/blog/how-to-win-an-awwwards-site-of-the-day)). The curators of Godly, now called Recent, are described as favouring "craft over novelty" and "grid discipline, considered type scales and generous whitespace" ([Downgraf](https://www.downgraf.com/inspiration/godly-website-design-inspiration/)). Anthropic's own front-end guidance reaches the same point from the other side: "a single orchestrated moment… lands better than scattered effects" ([anthropics/skills](https://github.com/anthropics/skills/blob/main/skills/frontend-design/SKILL.md)). **[E]/[P]**

The most-copied solo portfolios each have exactly one signature. Dennis Snellenberg's hero pairs his animated name with one photo, and every button has a "magnetic" hover. The site won Site of the Day on 13 April 2023 ([Awwwards](https://www.awwwards.com/dennissnellenberg/); [Medium](https://halimshams.medium.com/5-portfolio-websites-that-will-blow-up-your-mind-3f375db2741e)). Cuberto's signature is a cursor that changes shape and can carry images or video. The studio open-sourced it as "Mouse Follower" ([Cuberto GitHub](https://github.com/orgs/Cuberto/repositories); [Killer Portfolio](https://www.killerportfolio.com/by/cuberto)). Bruno Simon's portfolio is a driveable 3D car world that won Site of the Year 2020. He chose a car because it is easier to steer than a first-person view ([Creative Bloq](https://www.creativebloq.com/news/3d-car-portfolio)). The principle transfers to Focus Design: one memorable idea, made with care. The techniques mostly do not. Magnetic buttons and cursors need a mouse, and most of Focus Design's buyers (renovation firms, B&Bs, wedding venues) will first see the site on a phone. WebGL worlds would contradict a studio that sells fast 600–1.200 € sites. **[P]/[S]**

The productized studios, which are Focus Design's real peers, get their premium feel from constraint. **Designjoy** shows two calls to action ("see pricing" or "book a 15-min intro call"), promises an average delivery of about two business days, and removes risk with "pause or cancel at anytime" ([designjoy.co](https://www.designjoy.co/)). Its reported price varies between $4,995 and $5,995 a month depending on the source, so treat the figure as unverified ([Ycode](https://www.ycode.com/blog/a-complete-guide-to-subscription-web-design-agencies); [1capture](https://www.1capture.io/blog/designjoy-review)). **Goodspeed** sells a landing page "live in five working days" from $2,500 fixed, with design, build, forms, tracking and two revision rounds included ([Goodspeed](https://goodspeed.studio/product/landing-page)). **Zero-Meeting Studio** offers a $499 one-page site delivered async in 48–72 hours, and its name is the whole proposition ([zeromeeting.site](https://www.zeromeeting.site/)). That last case matters most here: the name, the offer and the process are the same idea. "Focus Design" has a name that can do the same job, but on the current site it does not. **[P]**

Focus Design already has one advantage over the free-mockup competitors it most resembles. Several of them hide the real price behind a monthly plan, such as "free with a monthly plan from $300" ([CR Local Business](https://crlocalbusiness.com/free-website-mockup)) or "$0 upfront, $149/month" ([Double G](https://www.double-g-consulting.com/website-design.html)). The transparent ones state the price ("free homepage mockup first, $1,000 for a complete site", [First & Forward](https://firstandforwardmarketing.com/)) or say what happens if the prospect walks away: the homepage "is yours to keep, whoever builds it" ([Designgratuit](https://designgratuit.com/)). Focus Design already shows 600 €, 900 € and 1.200 € on the home, a 72-hour preview, a 30% deposit and payment of the balance only on approval (`src/config/site.ts`, `src/pages/index.astro`). In productized terms, that open price is already the premium signal. It needs to be louder, not surrounded by more decoration. The research found no Italian productized studio at this price level. The Italian studios that surfaced, such as Studio375 with two Site of the Day awards ([Awwwards](https://www.awwwards.com/Studio375/)), are award-driven agencies, not competitors. **[E]/[P]**

## Apple's punch is scale and stillness, with motion rationed

Third-party teardowns of Apple's product pages describe three layers. The first is static: "headlines are very large, body copy is short, and generous whitespace slows the reader down and makes each statement feel deliberate", with one point per section and whitespace as "the brand's primary chrome" ([Wensity](https://ui.wensity.com/blog/the-anatomy-of-apples-design-language); [getdesign.md](https://getdesign.md/apple/design-md)). The second is a **pinned stage**: a tall scroll container gives scroll distance while a sticky frame holds the product still and the content around it changes ([ddingpy](https://ddingpy.github.io/mynote/posts/building-an-apple-style-scroll-experience-like-the-macbook-neo-page)). The third is **scroll-scrubbed sequences**, which "demand video assets shot for scrubbing, not just watching" and are usually drawn frame by frame on a canvas ([Autonoma Digital](https://autonoma-digital.pages.dev/blog/cinematic-scroll/)). Restraint is part of the design. Practitioners note that pinned sequences suit products that justify minutes of attention, and that basic information must never sit behind a scroll effect ([DBS Website](https://www.dbswebsite.com/blog/how-to-design-a-website-like-apples/)). Cinematic scroll needs fallbacks "for reduced-motion, for slow connections" and for browsers without scroll timelines ([Autonoma Digital](https://autonoma-digital.pages.dev/blog/cinematic-scroll/)). Apple publishes none of this, so it is all reconstruction. **[P]**

Measured against those layers, Focus Design has copied Apple's palette but not its pacing. The "Mela" theme is an exact Apple-style token set: white, `#1d1d1f`, `#6e6e73`, `#f5f5f7`, `#e8e8ed` and one blue, `#0071e3`, with Geist set big and tight (`src/styles/global.css`, `focale/CLAUDE.md`). The kinetic layer exists too, in the form of a WebGL iPhone that rings in the Dynamic Island while enquiries slide down as iOS banners (`src/components/HeroScene.astro`). The missing piece is the first layer, one idea per screen. After the hero, the home runs eight more blocks: a site-analysis bar, three sector cards, a "Cosa ricevi" grid of five icon tiles plus a link tile, four steps, three price cards with a helper and a footnote, a studio block with three facts, six FAQs and a closing form. Most of them repeat the same anatomy: blue label, large heading, lead paragraph, grid, staggered reveal. Each block is clean on its own, but together they make the even, busy texture that reads as "lots of small details". Apple's own pages also alternate dark and coloured full-bleed sections between white ones. I observed this, but the research found no source documenting it, so treat it as an observation. **[E]/[S]**

The useful lesson from Apple is therefore not to add a scrubbed film. Lorenzo already removed the makeover film from the home on 22 November 2026 because it felt useless there (`src/pages/index.astro`, script comment). The lesson is to **slow the page down with scale**: fewer, bigger statements, and one pinned or full-bleed moment where the work fills the screen. Prices, process, FAQ and contact stay plain, scannable and outside any motion. **[S]**

## Without a founder, the offer, the work and the voice carry trust

The sources are clear that a visible person builds trust, and that matters here because the owner has ruled it out. Practitioner advice for web-design studios is a short founder's note with photo and title on the homepage ([JoshHall.co](https://joshhall.co/add-a-founders-note-to-your-homepage-to-get-more-web-design-clients/)). Designjoy's homepage says the business "is run entirely by Brett" ([designjoy.co](https://designjoy.co/)), and Snellenberg's hero is his own face ([Medium](https://halimshams.medium.com/5-portfolio-websites-that-will-blow-up-your-mind-3f375db2741e)). Declining that cue has a real cost: the most direct proof that a real person answers is gone. Zero-Meeting shows the gap can be filled differently. Its personality is a promise about process, not a face ([zeromeeting.site](https://www.zeromeeting.site/)). For Focus Design, the replacements have to be concrete and checkable. They are the written price, the 72-hour deadline, a one-line answer to "what if I say no", real client work explained as decisions, and the Milan location with the VAT number. The site already has most of these in some form, but the VAT number and phone are still `[DA COMPILARE]` placeholders in `src/config/site.ts`, and a studio with no face cannot afford blank legal details. Goodspeed's pattern for showing work fits a studio without a face: each project is presented with the layout, copy and CTA reasoning behind it, plus one outcome line such as "100% on Lighthouse SEO" ([Goodspeed](https://goodspeed.studio/framer-landing-page-examples/startup); [goodspeed.studio](https://goodspeed.studio/)). Free-preview competitors frame the mockup as "a true design made for your business, not a stock template" ([Motivent](https://motiventmarketing.com/offers/website-redesign-home-services/)). For Focus Design, the preview itself is the portfolio device. **[P]/[S]**

Personality without a face lives in the voice, and the sources agree it belongs in low-stakes places. Microcopy is "the voice of the product, speaking directly to the user in moments where clarity or encouragement is needed most" ([Justinmind](https://www.justinmind.com/blog/microcopy-improve-ux-with-these-10-examples/)). Calls to action written from the client's point of view beat "Learn more", and a slightly funny unsubscribe line is cited as personality placed safely ([Sarah Klongerbo](https://sarahklongerbo.com/blog/microcopy-in-writing/)). GitHub's "If you just want to say hi, that's cool too" is the classic warm contact line ([Designmodo](https://designmodo.com/microcopy/)). Focus Design's dry, confident tone suits this well. The headline states the promise plainly, and the wit goes under the price button, in the form confirmation, in the FAQ answer about saying no, and on the 404 page. A pun on "fuoco" in every heading would turn the signature into a gimmick. Two uses per page is the ceiling. **[P]/[S]**

The evidence on what hurts is stronger than the evidence on what helps. In NN/g's usability testing, **scrolljacking threatened control, discoverability, efficiency and task success**. On one page every participant became disoriented, and task-focused users had the least patience. Scrolljacks were tolerated only when they progressively revealed relevant information with few scroll actions per change ([NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/scrolljacking-101/)). NN/g's older guideline says scroll-triggered text animations delay users. They belong on secondary content only, should play the first time only, and "should not unduly draw attention away from the content" ([NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/scroll-animations/)). Parallax and scrolljacking can make people with vestibular disorders dizzy or nauseous ([NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/animation-duration/)). In Tuch and colleagues' study, covered by Google Research, **low visual complexity and high prototypicality** were rated most appealing, and complexity registered first, from 17 ms of exposure ([Google Research](https://research.google/blog/users-love-simple-and-familiar-designs-why-websites-need-to-make-a-great-first-impression/); [paper](https://research.google.com/pubs/archive/38315.pdf)). That study used static screenshots, so its support for "remove clutter, keep the layout familiar" is an inference about motion, not a test of it. Hero video evidence is weak and mixed. Unbounce reports a single test where adding a video raised conversions 216%, with no methodology, and still advises against autoplay as "an interruption technique that annoys people" ([Unbounce](https://workshops.unbounce.com/lesson/2-5-testing-your-hero-shot/)). A "2,000 pages" study claiming autoplay heroes convert about 7% worse has unclear provenance ([Digital Applied](https://www.digitalapplied.com/blog/landing-page-conversion-study-2000-pages-tested-2026)). No source measured whether a signature interaction raises leads for small-business buyers. The case for one rests on memorability and on the fact that Focus Design sells taste, so its own site is its demo. **[E]/[P]**

Some current details also match the 2025–2026 lists of generated-page tells. A label above every section heading, icon tiles in a grid, the same reveal on every block and an arrow after button labels all appear on those lists ([anthropics/skills](https://github.com/anthropics/skills/blob/main/skills/frontend-design/SKILL.md)). On the home, every section has an eyebrow ("Per chi lavoriamo", "Cosa ricevi", "Come funziona", "Prezzi", "Lo studio", "Domande", "Anteprima gratuita"). The "Cosa ricevi" block is five icon tiles, and `data-in` reveals are staggered across nearly every block (`src/pages/index.astro`). The site's own rules already forbid "identical card grids" and all-caps eyebrows (`focale/CLAUDE.md`). The labels are not in capitals, but they do the same template job. Removing them is the cheapest gain available: it lowers visual complexity and the AI-look score at the same time. **[E]/[S]**

## Speed is the product, so the wow must be composited and CSS-first

The site sells speed. The "Cosa ricevi" tiles promise clients "Punteggio Google sopra 90" (`src/pages/index.astro`), so a heavy or janky homepage would undermine the pitch on the studio's own site. Inspecting the current build shows where the weight sits. The home schedules the three.js chunk (**about 645 KB minified, about 160 KB gzipped** in `dist/_astro/`) and the **938 KB `iphone.glb` model** to download once the page is idle, and draws them at the first gesture. The preload skips Save-Data and low-memory devices but otherwise runs on every capable visit, phones included (`src/pages/index.astro`, `src/scripts/phone3d.ts`). That keeps WebGL off the first paint, which is good engineering. It still costs roughly a megabyte of data and main-thread work for a phone that is already drawn in HTML underneath. GSAP with ScrollTrigger adds about 112 KB minified. Lenis smooth scrolling runs on fine pointers (`src/scripts/smooth.ts`). The block reveal animates `clip-path` as well as `transform` (`src/styles/global.css`), which is outside the starter's "transform/opacity only" rule. The existing focus motif animates `filter: blur(12px)` on headings, so text is unreadable for up to 1.2 seconds, and only on `pointer: fine` devices, so it never appears on phones. **[E]**

The platform limits are well documented. Lighthouse flags non-composited animations, meaning those that trigger style, layout or paint, and notes they can increase CLS. Transform and opacity animations are excluded from CLS ([Chrome for Developers](https://developer.chrome.com/docs/lighthouse/performance/non-composited-animations)). Smooth-scroll libraries such as Lenis intercept wheel and touch input and interpolate scroll on the main thread. INP does not score scrolling, but that main-thread work can delay the taps and clicks INP does measure ([Aidxn](https://aidxn.com/engineering/lenis-smooth-scroll-vs-native-2026/)). Accessibility checklists add that scrolljacking makes navigation harder for assistive-technology users ([Webflow](https://webflow.com/accessibility/checklist/category/interactions)). WCAG 2.3.3, Animation from Interactions, is the AAA reference for letting users switch off non-essential motion ([W3C](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG21/Understanding/animation-from-interactions.html)). **[E]/[P]**

By November 2026 the browser platform covers most of what Focus Design needs without libraries. web.dev's October 2026 roundup lists **CSS scroll-driven animations** (`animation-timeline: scroll()` and `view()`) in Chrome and Edge 115+, Safari 26 and Firefox 159, which makes them Baseline Newly available ([web.dev](https://web.dev/blog/web-platform-10-2026)). Older trackers still showed Firefox unsupported, so the exact Firefox scope needs a check ([Caniuse](https://caniuse.com/wf-scroll-driven-animations)). WebKit has published a CSS-only guide, and the feature is an Interop 2026 focus area ([WebKit](https://webkit.org/blog/17818/announcing-interop-2026)). Separate "scroll-triggered" animations, which start at a scroll point and then run on time, are still only in W3C TAG review ([W3C TAG](https://tag-github-bot.w3.org/gh/w3ctag/design-reviews/1167)). **Same-document view transitions** became Baseline when Firefox 144 shipped them in October 2025 ([web.dev](https://web.dev/blog/same-document-view-transitions-are-now-baseline-newly-available)). **Cross-document view transitions**, the kind a static Astro site uses between pages, were still unsupported in Firefox in the latest sources found, from late 2025 ([web-features](https://web-platform-dx.github.io/web-features-explorer/features/cross-document-view-transitions); [Caniuse](https://caniuse.com/cross-document-view-transitions)). They degrade to a normal page load, so the downside is nil. GSAP, including ScrollTrigger and SplitText, has been free for commercial use since 30 April 2025 ([Webflow](https://webflow.com/blog/gsap-becomes-free)), but it is still JavaScript that the CSS route mostly removes. The safe pattern is the same throughout: put the animation inside `@supports (animation-timeline: view())` and `@media (prefers-reduced-motion: no-preference)`, animate only transform and opacity, and make the static fallback the finished state ([Builder.io](https://www.builder.io/blog/view-timeline); [skills.sh](https://www.skills.sh/s1gmamale1/apple-design-skills/apple-design-web)). **[E]/[P]**

| Technique | Cost on a phone | 2026 support | Verdict for Focus Design |
|---|---|---|---|
| Opacity/transform reveal, CSS, played once | Negligible, composited | Universal | Use, but on 2–3 secondary blocks, not all |
| `animation-timeline: view()` crossfade or scale | No JS, composited | Baseline Oct 2026 (Firefox scope to verify) | Use for the signature and the work stage, inside `@supports` |
| `position: sticky` stage, native scroll | None | Universal | Use once, ≤ 1.5 screens per item |
| Cross-document view transitions | No JS | Chromium and Safari; Firefox gets a normal load | Optional polish |
| `filter: blur()` animated | Paint-heavy, outside the starter rule | Universal | Replace with a crossfade between a pre-blurred and a sharp image |
| `clip-path` mask reveal | Not transform/opacity | Universal | Replace with opacity/transform |
| Lenis smooth scroll | Main-thread scroll | n/a | Remove |
| GSAP ScrollTrigger | About 112 KB minified | n/a | Only where CSS cannot express a timeline, on that page only |
| WebGL phone (three.js + glb) | About 1.1 MB after load | Capable devices | Keep the phone story; make the 3D layer desktop-only or drop it |
| Autoplay hero video, custom cursor, sound | Weight, distraction, desktop-only | n/a | Avoid |

## The signature: make the name do the work

"Focus" is a photographic word, and the studio's offer is photographic too: a blurry sense that the current site is not working, then a sharp picture of the new one within 72 hours. The signature should act that out once, at the moment the visitor arrives, and then come back as a quiet motif. **[S]**

**"Messa a fuoco" in the hero.** The phone in the hero shows the new homepage of a site from the visitor's sector, first out of focus. Four viewfinder corner marks, like a camera's autofocus frame, close in around the screen with a small scale and opacity change, and the screen snaps sharp. The existing story then continues: the phone rings and the enquiry banners arrive. The whole beat lasts under a second and plays once. Technically it is two stacked images of the same screen, with the blurred copy generated at build time (the project already has `sharp` as a dev dependency). The sharp copy fades in by opacity, and the corner marks move by transform. No `filter` is animated and no text is ever blurred. The headline renders immediately and should remain the largest element on first paint, which needs a Lighthouse check. Without JavaScript, with reduced motion or in an older browser, the visitor sees the sharp frame and the marks already in place. The effect works the same on touch and on desktop because it needs no hover. **[S]**

**The motif, three or four times and no more.** The same corner marks frame the real client project in the work section, where each screenshot sharpens as it enters through a `view()` crossfade. They frame the preview form, which can be called "il primo scatto". They appear on the 404, which can say "Questa pagina è fuori fuoco." One more use makes the name literal for keyboard users: the `:focus-visible` indicator styled as the four corner marks. The brand's mark then becomes the site's focus ring, as long as it stays at least as visible as the current 2 px outline. The existing heading blur on inner pages should be retired in favour of this motif, so that the name means one thing across the site. **[S]**

**Copy that carries it lightly.** All lines below are proposals in Italian, using only facts already in `src/config/site.ts`, and they need Lorenzo's approval. Hero: "Il tuo sito, a fuoco in 72 ore." with the subline "Ti mostriamo la homepage nuova prima di chiederti un euro." Dark band: "Da 600 €. Lo vedi prima di pagarlo." Under the price buttons: "L'anteprima è gratis. Se non ti convince, ci salutiamo." Form confirmation: "Ricevuto. Ci mettiamo a fuoco: entro 72 ore hai l'anteprima." FAQ, "E se non mi piace?": "Finisce lì." followed by the policy, which is still undecided: `[TODO: chi tiene l'anteprima se il cliente non procede]`. "Fuoco" appears at most twice per page. **[S]**

The steelman against this idea is that the ringing phone already is the signature, and that a second concept dilutes it. The answer is to merge them rather than choose. The focus pull is the first beat, "this is your new site, sharp", and the ring is the second, "and it makes you get calls". Together they tell the offer in two seconds. **[S]**

## Conclusion

The research changes the problem from "add excitement" to "stop spending excitement evenly". The site already pays for plenty of motion: WebGL, GSAP, smooth scrolling and reveals on almost every block. Visitors still remember none of it, because no single moment owns the screen or the name. The winners in both families the studio sits between, award portfolios and productized offers, are memorable for one thing each. For a studio that has chosen not to show a face, that one thing has to be the name and the offer, because nothing else on the page is unique to Focus Design. Removing clutter is also the only change the tested evidence supports directly. The signature is a reasoned bet, not a measured one.

The constraint is also an opportunity. A studio selling fast, clean sites for 600–1.200 € can show its competence most credibly by doing more with less code: a CSS-only signature, native scroll, and a Lighthouse score it can point to. If the focus pull lands, the same mechanism can become a reusable, client-specific "signature slot" in the starter, with each client getting its own derived motif. The open questions are empirical. They are whether Firefox's 2026 scroll-timeline support is complete, whether the headline stays the largest element on first paint, and whether preview requests per visit move at all. The site's traffic is small, so that last answer will be qualitative for months.
