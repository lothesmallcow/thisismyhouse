# Lead with proof, then repeat one ask

The site should use a medium-long homepage of about nine sections with a conventional structure, and its first two screens should do most of the work. The hero is split. On the left are a plain-Italian headline saying what the studio does and for whom, the promise "homepage preview in 72 hours, free", one orange button reading **"Voglio l'anteprima gratuita"**, and a one-line risk reducer. On the right is the WebGL iPhone showing a finished sector site. The top of the before/after makeover shows above the fold, and the same button repeats directly under the makeover. The action never changes: one label and one destination, appearing in the header, the hero, after the makeover, on every price card, and in a closing band with a 3–4 field form. WhatsApp and phone sit beside it as visibly weaker routes. On phones, a two-button bottom bar takes over once the hero button has scrolled out of view. For type, one family is enough. Use **Schibsted Grotesk**, self-hosted (45.7 KB): 17–18 px body at weight 400, 600 for buttons and labels, 700–800 for headlines, and nothing lighter than 400. The evidence is uneven. The firmest findings are where attention falls, tap-target standards, the penalty for light font weights, the cost of form fields and the cost of slowness. Section order, how often to repeat the CTA, and the value of 3D are practitioner convention or my own synthesis, and they are marked as such throughout. The 3D phone and the makeover have no independent evidence of lifting enquiries. They pay their way only by showing the product and by never delaying the headline or taking over the scroll. Every reassurance line also has to be literally true. The current terms take a 30% deposit when work starts, so "paghi solo se approvi" would be false. Only "the preview is free, the balance is due on approval" is accurate.

**How to read the evidence marks.** **[E]** means evidence-backed: a measured study, an official standard, or a measurement taken on the project's own files. **[P]** means practitioner or vendor evidence such as single A/B tests, agency case studies or observed conventions, which is directional only. **[S]** means synthesis: my judgment combining the above for this specific studio.

## Three quarters of attention lands in the first two screens

The strongest layout fact is where attention goes. In NN/g's 2018 eyetracking (120 participants, 130,000+ fixations on 1920×1080 screens), **57% of page-viewing time was above the fold and 74% within the first two screenfuls**, about 2,160 px. The drop after the fold has persisted since 2010 ([NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/scrolling-and-attention/)). NN/g adds two caveats. The study did not control for page length, and it measured viewing, not conversion. NN/g also found that **80% of fixations fall on the left half of the screen** ([NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/horizontal-attention-leans-left/)), and that a value proposition has to land within about ten seconds or visitors leave ([NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/how-long-do-users-stay-on-web-pages/)). The practical consequence is that the essential argument must be complete in the first two screens: what, for whom, proof, price signal and the action. Every later section has to earn its scroll. **[E]**

Page length is less settled than the fold data. Copyhackers' tests point both ways. A short page beat a long control by 13% for Crazy Egg and lifted Highrise paid signups by 102.5%, while a longer page gave +37.5% signups elsewhere ([Copyhackers](https://copyhackers.com/long-form-sales-page/)). Joanna Wiebe's explanation is awareness. Visitors who know little about the offer need a page that walks them through, and fully aware visitors need very little ([Copyhackers](https://copyhackers.com/how-to-write-a-sales-page/)). CXL draws the same conclusion from research showing people buy when they feel they understand the product ([CXL](https://conversionxl.com/long-form-or-short-form/)). Unbounce's 41,000-page benchmark measures something different. **Plain copy at a 5th to 7th grade reading level converts best, and difficult words are the factor most strongly tied to lower conversion** ([Unbounce](https://unbounce.com/conversion-benchmark-report/methodology/)). Lead-gen pages over 800 words converted about 33% worse than pages under 200 ([MarketingProfs](https://www.marketingprofs.com/charts/2017/31924/lead-gen-landing-page-benchmarks-conversion-rates-and-content-metrics)). Window fitters and B&B owners know their site is old but have not worked out what a redesign buys them. Taken together, the evidence argues for **many short sections in plain words rather than a few long ones**: about nine sections, each with a heading that states its takeaway. **[P]** for the length tests, **[S]** for the nine-section budget.

The hero has to say plainly what the studio offers, and it must not look finished. NN/g's homepage guidance says newer brands in particular need a tagline stating what the company does, because vague slogans leave visitors unsure what is on sale ([NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/top-ten-guidelines-for-homepage-usability/)). A value proposition should name the user's outcome, not the mechanism ([NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/powered-by-ai-is-not-a-value-proposition/)). The current headline, "Siti che fanno squillare il telefono", is vivid but fails that test. It names neither the sectors nor the free preview, which is the studio's most unusual promise. Harry Dry's five-part checklist (title, subtitle, visual, proof, CTA) is the most concrete practitioner frame for the first viewport ([Not My Job, summarising Marketing Examples](https://not-my-job.beehiiv.com/p/how-to-write-landing-pages-that-sell-above-the-fold)). The equally important constraint is the **illusion of completeness**. A first screen that looks complete makes people assume there is nothing below, so the fold should cut through a visible element ([NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/videos/illusion-completeness/); [Webflow](https://webflow.com/blog/breaking-the-illusion-of-completeness)). Two things follow. The hero should be capped at about 85–90% of the viewport rather than 100vh, and full-screen snapped scenes should stay out, because each one is a designed false floor. NN/g also advises against rotating heroes and auto-forwarding carousels ([NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/designing-effective-carousels/)). **[E]** for the principles, **[S]** for the 88vh cap.

Grid numbers are mostly convention. The one researched value is line length: **about 50–75 characters, implemented as roughly 65–70ch** ([Baymard](https://baymard.com/blog/line-length-readability)). On small screens the measure fights font size, so the mobile measure should be designed first ([Smashing Magazine](https://www.smashingmagazine.com/2014/09/balancing-line-length-font-size-responsive-web-design/)). Alternating section backgrounds work because of the common-region principle: a shared container reads as a group ([NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/common-region/)). Tone changes should therefore mark the boundaries of the argument rather than flip mechanically every section. A 12-column, 1,200–1,280 px container with 96–128 px desktop section padding is standard practice with no research behind it. **[E]** for line length, **[S]** for the rest.

Navigation evidence is clearer. NN/g's 179-participant study found that **hiding the main navigation cut discoverability almost in half and slowed tasks, with a larger effect on desktop**. Combo navigation, with some links visible and the rest behind a menu, performed best ([NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/hamburger-menus/)). For headers, NN/g recommends partially persistent ones that hide on scroll-down and return on any small scroll-up, with an opaque background and a 300–400 ms slide ([NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/sticky-headers/)). That points to five visible text links plus one CTA button on desktop. On phones, the menu button should be labelled "Menu" rather than shown as a bare icon. **[E]**

Two benchmark findings shape the offer layout. Productized design studios have converged on one skeleton: an offer with a risk reverser, then work proof, three steps, inclusions, a fixed price on the page, FAQ and a final CTA. Designjoy repeats "Pause or cancel anytime" from the hero downward and shows its price in the open ([Designjoy](https://www.designjoy.co/)). In Milan, published prices are rare. DSI Design is the outlier, at **€1,500+IVA for a five-page sito vetrina** ([DSI Design](https://www.dsidesign.it/realizzazione-siti-web-milano/)), while freelancers such as Andrea Lupi and Griffini route everything through a quote request ([Andrea Web Designer](https://www.andreawebdesigner.com/restyling-siti-web-milano/); [Griffini](https://griffinidesign.it/)). Graphik Design shows demo homepages so prospects can see the style before choosing ([Graphik Design](https://www.graphikdesign.it/realizzazione-siti-web-pmi-professionisti-milano.html)), which confirms locally that a "see it first" offer makes sense. Earlier research found that **voluntary cost transparency raised sales by up to about 20% through trust** ([INFORMS](https://www.informs.org/News-Room/INFORMS-Releases/News-Releases/New-Research-Finds-Cost-Transparency-Can-Increase-Sales-20)), and that buyers who cannot find a price go to competitors ([NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/show-price/)). Prices on the homepage are therefore both evidence-backed and a local differentiator. **[E]** for transparency, **[P]** for the competitor scan.

## One action, repeated after each proof, beats a menu of buttons

Repetition follows from the fold data and from how considered purchases work. The hero button catches visitors who arrive already convinced, for example through a referral or a "restyling sito B&B" search. Sceptics need the proof first. ContentVerve's often-cited test moved the CTA below a much longer value explanation and **lifted conversions 304%**. Michael Aagaard himself cautioned that copy length changed at the same time, so placement alone does not explain the result ([Econsultancy](https://econsultancy.com/six-useful-case-studies-on-where-to-place-your-cta-to-maximise-conversions/)). The defensible reading is "add the CTA after proof", not "remove it from the hero". No published test varies the number of repetitions on a service homepage. An Unbounce analysis of 18,639 pages found **13.5% conversion for single-CTA pages against 10.5% for pages with many** ([MarketingProfs](https://www.marketingprofs.com/charts/2018/33935/what-converts-web-landing-page-benchmarks-and-trends-infographic)). That finding is about competing goals, not repetition. Repeating one action at each decision point is the consensus. Placing equally loud alternatives side by side is what hurts, and Hick's-law costs shrink when visual hierarchy makes one option dominant ([YouGov](https://yougov.com/articles/33184-present-options-hierarchically-address-consumers-c)). **[P]**

Button design is about isolation and size, not hue. "Red beats green" results are contrast effects: the button wins because it is the only thing of its colour ([EyeQuant](https://www.eyequant.com/blog/button-color-on-websites)). The project's identity already reserves orange `#ff6a1f` for actions. Measured with the WCAG formula, **navy `#0b1b3f` text on that orange is 5.9:1, while white text on it is 2.86:1 and fails**, so the navy label is correct. The same arithmetic rules out the stock WhatsApp style of white text on `#25d366` green, at 1.98:1. Any WhatsApp button should be outlined or white, with a navy label and the green glyph. **[E]** A CXL test on FreshEgg's homepage (10,000+ visits) found **ghost buttons drew 20% fewer clicks** than solid ones, which suits outlined buttons for secondary actions only ([CXL](https://cxl.com/blog/ghost-buttons/)). **[P]** Tap targets have hard floors: WCAG 2.2 AA requires 24×24 px, AAA 44×44 px, Apple 44 pt and Material 48 dp ([W3C](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/target-size-minimum.html); [Front-End Checklist](https://frontendchecklist.io/rules/accessibility/touch-targets)). Steven Hoober's grip observations found **49% of people using a phone one-handed** ([UXmatters](https://www.uxmatters.com/mt/archives/2013/02/how-do-users-really-hold-mobile-devices.php)), which puts the thumb zone at the bottom of the screen. **[E]**

Copy research supports specificity more than any pronoun rule. ContentVerve's change from "your" to "my" ("Start my free 30-day trial") reportedly **lifted sign-ups 90%** ([Leadpages](https://leadpages.com/blog/landing-page-button-text-fix)). The same switch **cut conversions 24.95% on a payment page** ([Review42](https://resources.review42.com/call-to-action-tips/)). The lever that holds up is a label naming what the visitor gets. Generic labels such as "Get started" stop users ([NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/get-started/)). "Voglio l'anteprima gratuita" is both first-person and specific, so it is a safe choice either way. Labels such as "Invia", "Contattaci" and "Scopri di più" should disappear. **[P]** The homepage's sector chooser currently gives each sector its own button label. The destination can stay sector-specific (`?settore=`), but the words should stay the same so that repetition never reads as a new choice. **[S]**

Microcopy under the button carries anxiety reduction to the point where hesitation happens. MECLABS's heuristic subtracts anxiety from the likelihood of conversion and recommends placing each fix next to the concern it answers ([MarketingExperiments](https://marketingexperiments.com/conversion-marketing/friction-and-anxiety-in-your-marketing-process-defining-the-difference)). The only quantified lift for "no credit card required"-style lines is a vendor projection of 5–15%, and the same source warns that **reassurance that is not literally true backfires** ([Atticus Li](https://atticusli.com/blog/posts/optimizely-10-high-impact-ab-tests-to-launch/)). This is where the deposit matters. The offer is "free preview, balance only on approval". The site's own terms ask for 30% when the build starts, so microcopy must say "l'anteprima è gratis; il saldo solo a sito approvato" and never "paghi solo se ti piace". Guarantee research supports the same caution. Generous claims from an unfamiliar seller are believed less than narrow ones ([ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/286120810_Do_Consumers_Perceive_Money-Back_Guarantees_as_Believable_The_Effects_of_Money-Back_Guarantee_Generosity_Store_Name_Familiarity_and_Perceived_Price)). **[P]/[S]**

Direct channels matter more in Italy than in the markets where most of these tests were run. Figures for WhatsApp reach vary with the measure: **90.1% of Italian internet users** in one survey ([Agenda Digitale](https://www.agendadigitale.eu/mercati-digitali/whatsapp-nelle-aziende-italiane-come-cambia-il-rapporto-coi-clienti/)) and **installed on 78.5% of Italians' devices** in 2022 Audicom data ([9colonne](https://www.9colonne.it/435011/whatsapp-messenger-and-telegram-the-most-used-chats-by-italians)). No rigorous test compares click-to-WhatsApp with forms for local services. The nearest data point is a vendor trial in a different market (Property Finder, UAE), which reported **+44% mobile leads** ([Property Finder](https://www.propertyfinder.ae/hub/?p=703)). WhatsApp therefore belongs everywhere as the secondary route, never as a rival primary. **[P]** All the evidence for sticky bottom bars is e-commerce and agency-reported. A bottom-placed sticky CTA beat a top one with **+27% clicks and +7% conversion**, though revenue per visitor fell ([Blend Commerce](https://blendcommerce.com/blogs/ab-tests-shopify/better-placed-sticky-add-to-cart-product-page-conversion)). Another sticky test was not significant ([Blend Commerce](https://blendcommerce.com/blogs/ab-tests-shopify/10-increase-in-conversion-rate)). The bar is a reasonable hypothesis backed by the grip data, not a proven lift. **[P]**

Forms are where friction can be measured. HubSpot's analysis of 40,000+ landing pages found **single-line fields cost little, while text areas, multiple drop-downs and phone or address fields lowered conversion** ([HubSpot](https://blog.hubspot.com/blog/tabid/6307/bid/6746/which-types-of-form-fields-lower-landing-page-conversions.aspx)). Baymard found users hesitate over unexplained phone fields and sometimes abandon them ([Baymard](https://baymard.com/blog/explain-phone-number-field)). **[E, observational]** Multi-step forms carry the loudest claims and the weakest sources. Venture Harbour's **+743%** came from a company that sold multi-step form software ([Venture Harbour](https://ventureharbour.com/cro-case-studies/)). The popular "86% better" figure attributed to HubSpot could not be found in any HubSpot source. **[P]** At this studio's traffic, an A/B test of the form would take months to reach significance. Field-level drop-off analytics and a few recorded sessions will teach more. **[S]**

On pricing, Ariely's Economist experiment is the classic demonstration of anchoring. A decoy print-only option moved print-plus-web choices **from 32% to 84%**, though the choices were hypothetical ([Think Insights](https://thinkinsights.net/strategy/decoy-effect)). The honest way to use the effect is a genuine top tier ("Su misura") that anchors the recommended middle plan, with one CTA per card. A plan designed only to be rejected is not acceptable. The badge must also be true: with no clients yet, "Consigliato" is honest and "Il più scelto" is not. **[P]/[S]**

## A news-media grotesque at 17 pixels serves 55-year-old eyes

Legibility research narrows the choice less than people expect, but it rules some things out firmly. Wallace and colleagues tested 16 fonts with hundreds of readers. **Each person's fastest font was 35% faster than their slowest, but which font was fastest varied by person, and preference did not predict speed** ([Adobe Research](https://research.adobe.com/publication/towards-individuated-reading-experiences-different-fonts-increase-reading-speed-for-different-individuals); [UCF](https://iems.ucf.edu/study-shows-personalized-fonts-speed-up-reading-maintain-comprehension/)). NN/g's summary is titled "no single answer" ([NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/best-font-for-online-reading/)). Among ordinary text faces, then, the family can be chosen for character. Weight and size are where the research is firm. Burmistrov's eyetracking found **light and ultralight weights less legible than regular and bold in both polarities, with higher cognitive load**, and older users rated ultralight lowest ([Burmistrov, NordiCHI 2016](https://flatisbad.com/publications/Burmistrov-NordiCHI16.pdf)). The owner's earlier rejection of Archivo wide light (weight 300) matches this finding. A 2024 poster found lighter weights read slightly faster, but the difference was not significant ([VSS 2024](https://www.visionsciences.org/presentation/?id=1032)). **[E]**

Size follows from vision science. Legge and Bigelow place fluent reading between about 0.2° and 2° of visual angle in x-height, and reading speed drops sharply below the critical size ([Legge & Bigelow, PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3428264)). Here is the calculation for an iPhone at 33 cm. A 16 px font with an x-height of 0.53 em gives about 1.4 mm, or roughly 0.24°, which is near the bottom edge of that range. Presbyopic owners aged 50–65 hold the phone further away and need more. That supports **17–18 px body text in a face with an x-height of at least 0.50 em**. **[E]** for the range, **[S]** for the conversion to phone pixels. On trust, an AMCIS study of a simulated bank site found that **serif and sans-serif both produced more trust than script or monospace faces** ([AIS eLibrary](https://aisel.aisnet.org/amcis2008/215)). A sans face does not cost credibility. Novelty faces do. **[E]**

The other constraint is not looking AI-generated or template-built. No study shows visitors detect this, but the mechanism is easy to see. AI design guidance now tells models to avoid Inter, Roboto and Space Grotesk ([skillselion mirror of Anthropic's frontend-design skill](https://skillselion.com/skills/smithery.ai/anthropics-frontend-design)). Instrument Serif has moved from a niche DTC choice to the default accent of LLM-assisted sites ([UGA AgTech Data](https://agtechdata.uga.edu/ai-has-come-for-serif-fonts/)). Models steered away from the first tier land on a second tier (Satoshi, Manrope, Plus Jakarta, DM Sans, Outfit, Bricolage), which is becoming the new template look. **[P]/[S]** Licensing removes several popular choices. Fontshare's Satoshi, General Sans and Switzer are under ITF's Free Font License, not the OFL, and may not be redistributed ([licenseorg](https://licenseorg.com/guide/fonts/fontshare)). **[E]**

Within these constraints, **Schibsted Grotesk** is the best fit. Bakken & Bæck designed it for the digital interfaces of a Scandinavian news group ([RightFont](https://rightfontapp.com/family/schibsted+grotesk)), so it reads as information rather than startup. It is OFL-licensed and rarely seen on templates. Measured on the Fontsource package, its upright latin variable file is **45.7 KB with an x-height of 0.527 em**, among the largest of the non-template candidates. Its weight axis **starts at 400, so a light weight cannot be shipped by accident**, and it exposes tabular figures for euro prices ([npm](https://www.npmjs.com/package/@fontsource-variable/schibsted-grotesk)). The Google latin subset already covers à è é ì ò ù, the ’ apostrophe, « », and €, so latin-ext can be dropped. **[E]** for the measurements, **[S]** for the choice. The site currently loads Bricolage Grotesque in two files and declares a display scale up to 9 rem (144 px). Switching to Schibsted removes the face the owner rejected. Capping the hero headline near 4.5 rem keeps the CTA above the fold on a 1366×768 laptop. **[S]**

The fallbacks are Funnel Display with Funnel Sans (about 34 KB for both; the designer is unverified), Host Grotesk (20 KB, with widths that stay the same across weights), or Atkinson Hyperlegible Next if older readers outweigh everything else. Use one family, with weights at 400, 600 and 700–800. Avoid a serif accent word, which reads as a template tell, and avoid wide display cuts, which split long words like "ristrutturazioni" at 390 px. **[S]**

## The 3D phone earns its place only by showing the product

There is **no independent A/B test of a 3D or WebGL hero against a static one**. The evidence that exists comes from vendors and measures engagement. Navattic's 2025 benchmarks cover 28,000 demos ([Navattic](https://navattic.com/report/state-of-the-interactive-product-demo-2025)), and earlier work found that demos placed higher on the page drew **3.5× the engagement** ([Navattic](https://www.navattic.com/blog/increase-conversions-for-interactive-demos)). The most useful comparison comes from a 2,000-page vendor study. **An animated hero gave +5% over a static image, while a single concrete-stat hero gave +18%** ([Digital Applied](https://www.digitalapplied.com/blog/landing-page-conversion-study-2000-pages-tested-2026)). The phone should therefore carry or sit beside a concrete promise ("anteprima in 72 ore, da 390 €"). Its screen should show the outcome, such as a real sector site or a "nuova richiesta di preventivo" notification, rather than rotate for its own sake. **[P]**

The reference sites all keep the action out of the 3D's way. Apple runs its scroll-driven product films with a persistent Buy button in a sticky sub-nav. Stripe puts text and CTA on the left with mockups on the right and moves its Three.js globe further down the page ([Stripe](https://stripe.com/blog/globe)). Linear puts the headline and CTA first and the product image under them, and a teardown still faults its hero for naming a category rather than an outcome ([Landing Doctor](https://landingdoctors.com/teardowns/linear-app)). In each case the headline and CTA sit above or beside the visual, never below it. **[P]** (page structures partly from recall; they need a live check)

Performance is the real risk. In an Italian A/B test, **Vodafone's 31% better LCP produced 8% more sales** on visually identical pages ([web.dev](https://web.dev/case-studies/vitals-business-impact)), and median mobile Total Blocking Time across the web is already **1,916 ms, nearly ten times the "good" threshold** ([Web Almanac 2025](https://almanac.httparchive.org/en/2025/performance)). **[E]** A `<canvas>` is not an LCP candidate, so a WebGL-only hero hands LCP to the headline. That is fine only if the headline renders without waiting for JavaScript. Three.js parsing still costs interactivity on the mid-range Android phones many owners carry. The established pattern is to start with a static poster image (eager, `fetchpriority="high"`) and load the scene after idle, cap the pixel ratio at 2, pause rendering off-screen, show a still frame under `prefers-reduced-motion`, and fall back gracefully when WebGL is unavailable ([MengTo skill](https://www.ui-skills.com/skills/mengto/webgl-landing-steering/); [Hedrick](https://hedrick.io/post/spline-3d-guide)). Reduced motion matters for this audience: **35.4% of US adults aged 40 and over show vestibular dysfunction** ([PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19468085)). NN/g's testing found that scrolljacking threatens control, discoverability and task success ([NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/scrolljacking-101/)). **[E]**

The makeover is the studio's strongest proof because it demonstrates the exact transformation being sold. Practitioners agree that **"one strong comparison does more than six weak ones"**, that both frames must match in size and crop, and that a before/after without hard facts "is only a showcase" ([Divimode](https://divimode.com/?p=1490349); [Phenomenon Studio](https://phenomenonstudio.com/article-faq/where-can-i-find-portfolio-examples-of-website-redesign-agencies/)). No conversion data exists for sliders against static pairs. **[P]** With no clients, the facts must be verifiable build facts, such as page weight, load time and Lighthouse score, not invented KPIs. The demo must be labelled "Progetto dimostrativo", because the Omnibus rules transposed by D.Lgs. 26/2023 ban fake reviews and the same logic applies to fake clients ([Il Sole 24 Ore](https://ntplusdiritto.ilsole24ore.com/art/la-direttiva-omnibus-nuove-regole-poste-tutela-consumatori-AEsGCvGD)). **[E]/[S]** Two moving set pieces in one viewport compete with each other and with the button, since motion captures attention faster than static content ([NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/animation-usability/)). The phone and the makeover each need their own screen, and the phone should go still when the makeover enters view. **[E]/[S]**

## Conclusion

The research changes the brief's emphasis in one important way. Effort usually goes into the first impression and the "wow". For this audience, the work that matters most is consistency: the same action, the same words and the same true reassurance at every point where a cautious owner might decide. The 3D phone and the makeover are worth keeping, because they are the closest thing the studio has to product demos and no competitor in Milan offers a "see it before you pay" experience. But they are evidence of craft, not drivers of enquiries. They must therefore never delay the headline, block the scroll or share a screen with each other. The biggest risks are not visual. One is copy that overstates the risk reversal against the 30% deposit. Another is a headline that names a feeling rather than the sectors and the free preview. A third is display type too large to leave the button above the fold on a laptop.

What remains unknown should be tested with people rather than with traffic the site does not yet have. Nobody has measured CTA repetition counts, sticky bars on service sites, WhatsApp against forms, the value of a 3D hero, or Italian-language button copy. At the studio's likely volume, five-second tests of the hero with renovation and B&B owners will answer more than an A/B test could. So will a week of field-level form analytics and a count of approved previews per enquiry channel.

## Blueprint

The current build already follows several of these recommendations: the makeover sits second, there are prices on the homepage, a two-button sticky bar and a single action label in the hero. The changes are mainly to the headline, the type family and scale, the CTA under the makeover, uniform labels, the microcopy, the voice (from "noi" to a named "io") and form details.
